The Trio Behind the Glow Blend

BPC-157 comes from a sequence in human gastric juice. The synthetic version is studied for tissue healing, gut health, and anti-inflammatory balance. It may support angiogenesis which is the growth of new blood vessels. This helps move nutrients to areas that need repair.

TB-500 is a short fragment of thymosin beta 4. Thymosin beta 4 is a naturally occurring protein in human plasma. TB-500 helps cells move to injured sites. It supports actin dynamics which is key for cell migration and organization during healing.

GHK-Cu is a copper peptide found in human plasma and saliva. It can bind copper and act as a signaling molecule. Research links GHK-Cu to collagen stimulation, elastin production, and improved skin elasticity. It also shows antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects.

How To Dose and Track Your Peptide Injections

A peptide protocol is a plan, not a single dose. It sets a start phase, a steady phase, and a review point. This helps you measure what works and what needs to change. Always work with a clinician to set dose, frequency, and cycle length.

Safety, Side Effects, and Who Should Avoid

Most people tolerate peptides well when used under medical guidance. Still, side effects can occur. Mild effects include headache, nausea, and irritation at the injection site. These usually pass within a short time.

Stop and speak with a clinician if you see severe redness, swelling, fever, or a rash. People who are pregnant or nursing should avoid peptide therapy. People with active cancer or serious chronic disease should only consider peptides with specialist oversight. Always use products from a reputable source and store them as directed.
