Explanation

GLP-1 agonists (like semaglutide and liraglutide) have transformed diabetes care and weight management by slowing stomach emptying and reducing hunger. While many patients appreciate the weight-loss effects, some report zero appetite, leading to unintended malnutrition. This guide explains why appetite suppression occurs, how to spot early warning signs of undernutrition, and practical GLP-1 and loss of appetite hacks to keep you nourished and healthy.

Why GLP-1s Can Cause Near-Total Appetite Loss

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) medications work by:

Stimulating insulin release in response to high blood sugar

Slowing gastric emptying, so you feel full longer

Acting on brain centers that regulate hunger

In clinical trials (JAMA, 2020; NEJM, 2021), up to 30% of users report minimal to zero hunger. For some, what starts as helpful satiety becomes problematic, leading to:

Rapid, unintended weight loss

Vitamin and mineral deficiencies

Weakness, fatigue, dizziness

Recognizing Early Signs of Malnutrition

Undernutrition can sneak up on you. Watch for:

Unplanned weight loss >1–2 lbs/week

Chronic fatigue, muscle weakness

Hair thinning, brittle nails

Dry skin or sores that heal slowly

Lightheadedness when standing

Irritability, poor concentration

If you notice these symptoms, act quickly to prevent complications such as electrolyte imbalances or muscle wasting.

Heart palpitations

Sudden, significant weight loss (>5% body weight in 1 month)
