What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a mineral-rich substance formed over centuries from decomposed plant matter in mountainous regions (such as the Himalayas). It's been used for centuries in Ayurvedic medicine for its:

High fulvic acid content

Essential trace minerals (iron, magnesium, zinc, selenium)

Antioxidant-packed humic acids

These components work together to support energy production, cognitive function, and resilience to stressors.

Choosing and Using Shilajit Safely

Not all Shilajit products are created equal. To ensure quality and safety:

Source matters

Choose products sustainably harvested from reputable mountain regions.

Look for independent third-party testing (heavy metals, microbial contamination).

Standardized extracts

Opt for preparations standardized to contain a reliable percentage of fulvic acid (typically 60–80%).

Avoid unprocessed raw resin without proper purification.

Recommended dosage

Commonly, 300–500 mg per day, often split into morning and early afternoon doses.

Start at the lower end and adjust based on how you feel, under doctor supervision.

Timing

Take with meals or blended into smoothies to enhance absorption.

Avoid late-evening dosing if you're sensitive to increased energy at night.

Duration

Many users note benefits within 4–6 weeks.

Consult your physician about longer-term use and periodic breaks.

Potential Side Effects and Precautions

Shilajit is well tolerated by most people, but a few considerations apply:

Individuals with gout or kidney stones should use caution due to mineral content.

Those on anticoagulants or diabetes medications should discuss interactions.

Allergic reactions are rare but possible—discontinue use if you notice rash or digestive upset.

Always ensure you're not exceeding recommended dosages.
