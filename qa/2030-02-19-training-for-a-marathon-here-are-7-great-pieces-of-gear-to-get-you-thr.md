# Training for a Marathon? Here Are 7 Great Pieces of Gear to Get You Through Race Day.

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

TAKING ON 26.2 miles takes a lot of focus, grit, hard work, and sacrifice. The last thing that you want is for your marathon running gear to be the reason that you don’t make it the full distance, or not cross the finish line by your target time. So one of the first steps to running a marathon, after securing your bib of course, is making sure that you have the right clothing and equipment to support you on your journey of physical endurance—more than just the perfect running shoes.

If you’re taking on your first marathon you will learn what veterans already know, every ounce counts when it comes to running this many miles. Both in training and during the event itself, so don’t expect us to throw a bunch of bulky items at you. Or try to push you towards a flashy high-tech option just because it looks cool. These are the essentials, designed to keep you seeing clearly, moving well, protected from the sun, and hydrated so that you can keep pushing forward.

What to Consider

Materials

[Get You Through](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/)

Running for hours on end, pushing yourself to the brink physically and mentally, is by nature uncomfortable. So it’s important to find items that will bring you comfort, or at least not make you more uncomfortable, in this time of suffering. So be sure to consider materials and how much contact they will have on your skin over the course of your running.

Size

One of the most important factors to make sure that your running gear is comfortable is proper sizing. For running shoes, never buy the same size of running shoes as your regular sneakers, as you will always need extra room. Expect your feet to swell quite a bit during your training, and of course during the actual event. The last thing you want is blisters or cramped feet caused by having shoes that are too tight.

Breathability

The side effects of putting out massive amounts of energy include heat and swelling, both of which can be real race-stoppers if not dealt with. Be sure that all of the gear that you are wearing, from your head to your toes, encourages airflow. Look for breathable materials like nylon and polyester to be in any clothes you pick up, since these fabrics also have moisture-wicking properties. You’ll notice that none of the picks here cover too much of the body either, and we like it that way.

Weight

As we stated above, every ounce matters on marathon day. So it’s important to consider how much value each piece of gear brings and if it’s worth the extra load, however small. The race day picks we made below take this into consideration, and are more lightweight than other offerings. Of course, when it comes to training, adding weight during your runs is a way to increase the difficulty level without taking more time out of your schedule. For anyone who has trained for a marathon knows, the time commitment made is pretty substantial. This can be done with a weighted vest, or even a hydration vest, like the one we have listed below.

This is the kind of shoe that excels as a daily trainer, as well as a long-distance running option. They hold up great in that 5K and 10K range, but you probably will want to upgrade to more cushion (like our pick below) when going the full event distance.

The right running shorts can be a game changer when preparing to go longer distances in preparation for a marathon. Sure, you might have been able to do a couple miles in your regular training shorts, but once you cross that 5K range the resistance really starts to become a factor. It should come as no surprise that you don’t want an overly long inseam, especially nothing past the knees. Personally I go for the 5-inch inseam, which provides coverage, feels light on the body, and shows off a little thigh. Because why hit leg day and do marathons if you’re not going to reap the visual rewards?

Lululemon is a brand that has built a reputation on making good looking athleisure clothing that mixes performance with comfort. So it wasn’t shocking to me, over the course of trying many a running short, that those qualities were ever-present in the Pace Breakers. I’ve owned both the lined (that comes with an additional compression layer for a few dollars more) and the unlined versions of these shorts. For running I have always opted for the unlined pair, as they provide more freedom and less resistance on the road.For a running shirt to match these shorts, this is where personal preference and weather come more into play. No matter the temperature, whether it’s blistering hot or absolutely freezing where you live, proper and constant hydration is critical. While you might get more biological clues to take a sip of water in the summer, it’s important to track how much water you are taking in, especially when wearing moisture-wicking materials that might make you feel like you haven’t sweat as much as you actually have. Not everyone carries a hydration vest on marathon day, as many will rely on the water stations along the route for their needs.

I like to have a vest for training, as there are no hydration stations just hanging around in my neighborhood, and the last thing I want to do is sabotage my runs by not getting enough water. I also have never liked holding my water bottle, strap or not, as I know it messes with my form. I’ve opted to wear a hydration vest on event runs when I don’t want to worry about the stations not being frequent enough. I like this option from Arc’teryx as it’s lightweight, fits snug against the chest, and adjusts to the rest of the body easily. I liked that it comes with two 500mL flasks, so that you don’t have to find a water bottle that fits with your new vest.
