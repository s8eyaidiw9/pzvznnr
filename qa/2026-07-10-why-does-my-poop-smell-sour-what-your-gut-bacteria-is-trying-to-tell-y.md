What causes a sharp, sour-smelling stool?

A sharp, sour stool odor typically results from gut bacteria fermenting undigested carbohydrates. Common causes include:

Why Does My Poop Smell Sour? What Your Gut Bacteria Is Trying to Tell You About Your Diet

Noticing a sour or acidic smell in your stool can be uncomfortable and confusing. While poop doesn't usually smell pleasant, a sharp, sour odor often points to how your digestive system is breaking down food—especially sugars and carbohydrates. In many cases, this smell is a signal from your gut bacteria that something in your diet isn't being fully digested.

The good news? A sour smell is often related to common, manageable issues. Still, it's important to understand what your body may be telling you and when it's time to speak to a doctor, especially if symptoms persist or worsen.

What Does "Sour-Smelling" Poop Usually Mean?

A sour smell typically comes from acidic byproducts created during digestion. When food isn't properly broken down or absorbed, it can ferment in the intestines. This fermentation produces acids and gases that change the smell of stool.

The most common reasons include:

Malabsorption of carbohydrates

Imbalances in gut bacteria

Food intolerances, especially lactose intolerance

Temporary digestive upset from diet changes or illness

Most of these causes are not dangerous on their own, but they can affect your comfort, nutrition, and quality of life if ignored.

The Role of Gut Bacteria in Stool Smell

Your digestive tract is home to trillions of microorganisms, collectively known as your gut bacteria or gut microbiome. These bacteria help:

Break down food your body can't digest on its own

Produce certain vitamins

Support immune function

When everything is balanced, digestion runs smoothly. But when undigested food—especially sugars—reaches the large intestine, bacteria ferment it. This process creates:

Short-chain fatty acids (which can smell sour)

Gas

Changes in stool consistency and odor

A sour smell is often a sign that your gut bacteria are working overtime on food that should have been absorbed earlier in digestion.

Malabsorption: A Common Underlying Issue

Malabsorption happens when your body can't properly absorb nutrients from food. Instead of being absorbed in the small intestine, nutrients move into the colon, where bacteria ferment them.

Malabsorption can involve:

Carbohydrates (most common cause of sour stool)

Fats (often causes greasy or very foul-smelling stool)

Proteins (less common, but possible)

Signs of Malabsorption May Include:

Sour or unusually strong-smelling stool

Loose stools or diarrhea

Bloating and gas

Unintended weight loss

Fatigue or nutrient deficiencies over time

Malabsorption can be temporary (such as after a stomach bug) or ongoing, depending on the cause.

Lactose Intolerance: A Leading Cause of Sour Stool

One of the most frequent causes of sour-smelling poop is lactose intolerance.

Lactose is the natural sugar found in milk and dairy products. To digest it, your body needs an enzyme called lactase. If you don't make enough lactase, lactose passes undigested into the colon.

There, gut bacteria ferment it, producing acids and gas.

Common Symptoms of Lactose Intolerance:

Sour-smelling stool

Diarrhea after consuming dairy

Bloating

Stomach cramps

Symptoms can range from mild to severe and may worsen with larger amounts of dairy. Lactose intolerance is very common and can develop gradually in adulthood.

Beyond lactose intolerance, several dietary factors can lead to sour-smelling stool:

High sugar intake (especially fructose or artificial sweeteners)

Large amounts of processed carbohydrates

Sudden increase in fiber, particularly fermentable fibers

Drinking fruit juices or smoothies in excess

These foods can overwhelm digestion, leading to fermentation by gut bacteria.

Could It Be Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)?

If sour-smelling stool comes with ongoing digestive symptoms like diarrhea, constipation, bloating, or abdominal discomfort that come and go, it may be helpful to check your symptoms with a free AI symptom checker to better understand what might be happening and whether professional evaluation is needed. IBS affects how the gut moves and processes food, often leading to malabsorption and changes in gut bacteria activity.

IBS commonly includes:

Diarrhea, constipation, or both

Bloating and abdominal discomfort

Changes in stool smell and consistency

Symptoms that come and go

This kind of self-check is not a diagnosis, but it can help you decide whether to talk with a healthcare professional.

When Sour-Smelling Stool Is More Concerning

While many causes are related to diet, there are situations where sour or unusual stool odor should not be ignored.

Why You Shouldn't Ignore Ongoing Symptoms

Occasional sour-smelling stool is usually harmless. But chronic symptoms can lead to:

Poor nutrient absorption

Fatigue

Ongoing digestive discomfort

Reduced quality of life

Getting clarity—especially when malabsorption or lactose intolerance is involved—can make a big difference in how you feel day to day.

The Bottom Line

Sour-smelling poop is often your body's way of saying that food, especially carbohydrates, isn't being fully digested. Most commonly, this is linked to gut bacteria, malabsorption, or lactose intolerance. While these issues are common and often manageable, persistent or severe symptoms deserve medical attention.

If something feels off or is interfering with your daily life, speak to a doctor. This is especially important if you notice warning signs that could indicate a more serious or life-threatening condition.

Listening to your gut—literally—can help you make smarter choices about your diet, your health, and when to seek professional care.
