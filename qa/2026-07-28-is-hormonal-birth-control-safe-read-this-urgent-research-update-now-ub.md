Hormonal birth control is a safe, effective method for preventing pregnancy, but it can cause side effects. Common side effects include nausea, headaches, breast tenderness, mood changes, spotting between periods, and changes in libido. Most side effects are mild and often resolve within 2-3 months as your body adjusts. However, some people may experience more serious risks such as blood clots, high blood pressure, or migraines with aura. Side effects vary depending on the type of hormonal contraceptive—pills, patches, rings, injections, implants, or IUDs—and your individual health profile.

If you're experiencing symptoms that concern you, it's important to understand what's happening in your body before deciding on next steps. Explanation

Hormonal birth control methods, like the pill, are used by many people to prevent pregnancy. These methods work by changing hormone levels in the body to stop ovulation, which is when an egg is released from the ovaries.

Safety and Effectiveness

Safe: Research shows that hormonal birth control is generally safe for most people. However, some might experience side effects like nausea or mood changes.

Effective: When used correctly, hormonal birth control is very effective in preventing pregnancy.
