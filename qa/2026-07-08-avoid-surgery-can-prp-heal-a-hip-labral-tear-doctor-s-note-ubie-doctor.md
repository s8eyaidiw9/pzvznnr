Can PRP Help Heal a Hip Labral Tear Without Surgery?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections are a promising non-surgical treatment for mild to moderate hip labral tears. PRP may reduce pain, improve joint function, and potentially delay or eliminate the need for surgery in many patients.

However, outcomes vary based on tear severity, PRP preparation techniques, and whether adjunct therapies like physical therapy are used. Understanding these factors—along with the risks and benefits—is essential before choosing this treatment path.

Explanation

A hip labral tear occurs when the ring of cartilage (the labrum) that cushions the hip joint is damaged. This can lead to pain, stiffness, and reduced mobility. While surgery is often recommended for significant tears, non-surgical options like platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections are gaining attention. Below, we explore whether PRP for hip labral tear can help you heal without going under the knife.

Recovery: You'll rest briefly, then gradually resume activities as advised.

Risks and Side Effects

PRP is generally safe because it uses your own blood. Possible side effects include:

Mild pain or discomfort at the injection site

Temporary increase in inflammation

Infection risk (very low if sterile technique is used)

Bleeding or bruising

No serious long-term complications have been commonly reported.
