# How to Build a Vegetable Garden

Mike McGrath started his garden for the same reason many guys do extraordinary things: to impress a beautiful woman.

He had spotted her at a party, where she told him all she wanted was to move out to the country with "a man who would grow her raspberries.” He was going to be that man. Hours later, he called a friend and asked him how the hell to grow raspberries.

Today, he’s a former editor at Organic Gardening and Men’s Health, as well as host of the nationally-syndicated radio show, You Bet Your Garden. He’s also married to the woman he set out to impress. “The difference between a good gardener and a poor gardener is that a good gardener doesn't give up,” McGrath says.

Here are McGrath's tried and true tips on how to grow a vegetable garden this summer—even if you live in a New York studio apartment.

Build A Strong Foundation

Even if you have an incredibly sun-lit house with wall-to-wall windows, you still won’t be able to grow food indoors without springing for artificial light. So if you try to grow anything heftier than a herb garden indoors, “I just hope the plants die quickly [to save you time],” McGrath says.

If you're a city dwellers, you have a couple of options. If you have a porch or backyard, plant inside a halved whiskey barrel with drainage holes in the bottom, or use the biggest clay pot you can find. Otherwise, reserve a plot in a community garden where you'll get a raised bed kit (a wooden box for your plants) that protects you from rampant weeds, pests, flooding, and—most importantly—crappy soil, as the borders let you build up a new foundation of dirt. “Plus, if you live in a urban area or if you have a house made before 1875, there is a good chance there is lead in your soil,” McGrath says.

If you live in a more rural area and have the room, build your own wood bed kit one foot high and four feet wide. It can be as long as you want, as long as you can reach your plants from either side of the bed.

“Plant roots need air,” McGrath says. "That’s the big secret." If you are stepping inside the bed, you are compacting the soil and suffocating your garden.

Pick The Right Soil

Don’t be tempted by the convenience of mass market bagged soil. It’s been sitting in the hot sun for months in shrink-wrapped plastic—how good do you think it really is?

Spring for high-quality compost, topsoil, and a soil-free mix, like perlite, from a local shop.

The topsoil should have a consistently of a well-wrung out wet sponge when you squeeze it, and it should smell like rich, black earth. If it has a chemical smell to it, move on says McGrath.

For a raised bed, mix together equal parts compost and topsoil, as well as one big bag of perlite. Go for a ratio of one-third compost, one-third soil-free mix, and one-third topsoil, if you’re using a barrel or pot.

Go For Easy Crops

McGrath says tomatoes are “the gateway drug of gardens.” A lot of people come into gardening wanting to grow them, when in reality they're one of the hardest plants to grow. A good rule of (green) thumb: first-timers should avoid any vegetable with vines.

[to Build](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/blog/4188755162.html)

Instead, start off with a mix of pepper plants, and don’t stress yourself out by trying to grow from seeds at first.

Don’t worry about what your buddies might think of your new hobby, also. “No one is going to challenge your manhood if you are growing hot peppers,” McGrath says. “They're the plant version of a gas grill or a pickup truck.”

Other easy-to-grow plants include snow peas and salad greens in the spring and fall, as well as cucumbers, zucchini, and bush-style string beans during warmer months.

Be Smart About Watering

You can’t go out and throw a gallon of water on a plant and call it a day, McGrath says. You want to your plant to grow deep roots, so that means slow, long, and infrequent watering.

“Plant roots are just like a guy’s muscles,” McGrath says. "Watering them deeply once a week is like taking them to the gym. It will make their roots grow deep and strong."

If you're unsure of water levels, poke a chopstick into the soil to measure the water line. Quite often, you’ll find that your plant’s roots are completely soaked.

“Let the plant work out for a couple days,” McGrath says. Then, after the soil has been completely dry for a day or two, reward it with a nice long drink.
