Why do I feel pain in my wrist joint when bending it?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Wrist pain when bending is often caused by arthritis, tendon inflammation (such as tendinitis or a less common syndrome affecting the wrist tendons), or injuries like sprains and fractures. Repetitive strain, carpal tunnel syndrome, and ligament damage can also trigger pain during wrist movement. Identifying the exact cause matters, because treatment varies widely — from simple rest and bracing to physical therapy or medical intervention.

Because wrist pain has many possible causes, guessing can delay proper care and prolong discomfort. Explanation

Feeling pain in your wrist when you bend it can have several possible causes. Arthritis is one common reason. It can happen when the joints in your wrist become inflamed, often due to wear and tear or repetitive movements. This condition can be more common in certain jobs that require a lot of hand use.

Another possible cause is a condition called Linburg-Comstock syndrome. This is a rare condition where the tendons in your wrist and hand are connected in an unusual way, which can cause pain when you move your wrist.

Injuries, like a fracture, can also lead to wrist pain. For example, a fracture in one of the small wrist bones, like the trapezoid bone, can cause pain and difficulty moving your wrist. This might happen if you fall or hit your wrist hard.
