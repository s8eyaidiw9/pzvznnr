Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Magnesium supplements come in several forms—oxide, citrate, glycinate, malate, and threonate—each differing in absorption rate, elemental magnesium content, and targeted health benefits. Magnesium citrate supports digestion, glycinate promotes sleep and relaxation, malate helps with muscle pain and energy, and threonate may enhance cognitive function. Choosing the right form depends on your health goals, tolerance, budget, and dosing preferences.

Below, you'll find detailed comparisons of each magnesium type, recommended dosages, safety considerations, and guidance on when to consult a healthcare provider.

Common Types of Magnesium Supplements

Below is a comparison of popular magnesium forms. Each has unique properties that make it more or less suitable depending on your goals.

Magnesium Oxide

Widely available

High elemental magnesium content per dose

Often used as a laxative

Lower absorption (<10%)

Likely to cause loose stools

Magnesium Citrate

Better absorption (25–30%)

Gentle laxative effect—useful for occasional constipation

May still cause digestive upset at higher doses

Magnesium Glycinate (Bisglycinate)

Highly bioavailable (up to 80%)

Gentle on the stomach—less likely to cause diarrhea

Promotes relaxation and better sleep

Often more expensive

Magnesium Malate

Combines magnesium with malic acid (involved in energy production)

May help with muscle pain and fatigue

Mild laxative effect in sensitive individuals

Magnesium Threonate

Crosses the blood-brain barrier effectively

May support memory and cognitive function

Lower elemental magnesium by weight

Pricier than other forms

Magnesium Sulfate (Epsom Salt)

Used in baths to soothe sore muscles

Absorbed through the skin (though data is limited)

Not ideal for oral supplementation

Magnesium Chloride

Moderate absorption (around 12%)

Available in oils/gels for topical use

Can taste bitter

Magnesium Orotate

May benefit heart health by supporting cardiac muscle cells

Limited research

Higher cost

How to Choose the Right Type

When selecting a supplement, consider these factors:

Your Primary Goal

Constipation relief → Magnesium oxide or citrate

Sleep and relaxation → Magnesium glycinate

Muscle pain/fatigue → Magnesium malate

Cognitive support → Magnesium threonate

Absorption & Tolerance

If you've had GI upset from magnesium before, opt for glycinate or malate.

Form & Convenience

Capsules vs. powders vs. topical oils: choose what fits your routine.

Powders (citrate, malate) can mix into water or smoothies.

Cost & Availability

Glycinate and threonate tend to cost more.

Oxide and citrate are budget-friendly and common in drugstores.

Dosage and Safety Considerations

Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)

Adult men: 400–420 mg/day

Adult women: 310–320 mg/day

Tolerable Upper Intake Level (supplements only)

Adults: 350 mg elemental magnesium/day

Excessive dosing can lead to diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramping.

Potential Drug Interactions

Certain antibiotics (tetracyclines, quinolones)

Bisphosphonates (for osteoporosis)

Some diuretics and heart medications

Always read labels for elemental magnesium content and follow dosing instructions. If you have kidney disease or other chronic conditions, talk to your healthcare provider before starting any supplement.

When to Seek Medical Advice

While magnesium supplements are generally safe for most people, certain symptoms or conditions may require professional evaluation. If you experience:

Severe muscle weakness or cramps

Persistent digestive issues

Irregular heartbeat

Signs of allergy (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
