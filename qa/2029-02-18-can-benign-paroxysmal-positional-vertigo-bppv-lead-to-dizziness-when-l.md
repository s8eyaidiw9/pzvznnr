Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a common inner ear disorder that causes sudden dizziness, and looking at a computer screen can make symptoms worse. This happens because your eyes and vestibular (balance) system work together to keep you oriented—when tiny calcium crystals in your inner ear become displaced, screen movement and visual scrolling can overwhelm this connection, triggering vertigo, nausea, or lightheadedness.

Explanation

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a condition that causes dizziness when your head changes position. Looking at a computer screen can make you feel dizzy if you have BPPV because the screen can trick your eyes and balance system.

Why Screens Can Make You Dizzy

Visually-Induced Motion: According to the first reference, screens can create a feeling of movement that confuses your brain. This is because your eyes see movement even though your body is still.
