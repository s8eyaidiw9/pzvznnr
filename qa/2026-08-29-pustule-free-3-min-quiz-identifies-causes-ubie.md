Generally, Pustule can be related to:

Subcorneal pustulosis is a skin condition where non-infected, pus-filled pustules or blisters form on the skin, especially in areas where the skin rubs together. The cause is unclear but may be linked to other systemic conditions or certain medications. It is not contagious or inherited. Adult women are at higher risk, but children can develop this condition too.

AGEP is a severe reaction, often caused by drugs, that appears as numerous small pustules on the skin with swelling and redness. It usually starts on the face and can spread to the trunk and limbs. The reaction often begins a few hours to days after taking the offending drug, most commonly antibiotics, antifungals, and antimalarials.

Eosinophilic pustular folliculitis is a chronic, non-infectious skin disorder characterized by recurring itchy, red or skin-colored bumps and pustules. The bumps mostly appear on the face, scalp, neck and trunk and may persist for weeks or months. It may be associated with HIV infection, various drugs, and some lymphomas.
