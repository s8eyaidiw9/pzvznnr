What can an IV infusion treat? An IV infusion can help with several health issues and conditions, including malnutrition, dehydration, vitamin deficiencies, pain relief, wound healing, and treatment after surgery. Whether you’re recovering from an injury or surgery, dealing with chronic illness or disease, or battling cancer or another disease that may require treatments, getting an IV infusion may help you recover more quickly and effectively so you can get back to your daily life quickly!

What is an IV infusion?

Intravenous (IV) infusions are fluid replacements administered directly into your vein via a needle. If you suffer from severe dehydration, an emergency is in progress, or you’re undergoing some sort of surgical procedure that requires immediate hydration, then getting an IV can be quite helpful. The fluids given to you through an IV will help replenish lost fluids faster than drinking water alone—and it doesn’t take much time to administer.

Most people will use an IV infusion to help fight severe dehydration. This is especially helpful if you have a condition that requires fluid administration throughout your day (like diabetes, kidney failure, or heart disease). When you’re administered fluids through an IV, they’re more quickly absorbed into your bloodstream than they would be if you were to drink them. This is due to their increased concentration.

Chronic fatigue syndrome

Vitamin B12, an essential vitamin for energy production, has been shown to reduce symptoms of chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS). Vitamin B12 can help boost your immunity and improve nerve function. Infusions have been shown to be effective at relieving headaches in sufferers of CFS. In addition to B12, there are many other vitamins that can help people living with CFS. B-Complex Vitamins have been shown to help sufferers of CFS feel better overall. These nutrients include thiamine, riboflavin, niacinamide, pantothenic acid, pyridoxine hydrochloride, and biotin. Each of these nutrients is needed by our bodies to make energy and regulate our metabolism. These vitamins also play a role in how we process carbohydrates, fats, and proteins, as well as synthesize DNA.

Fibromyalgia

Infusions are sometimes used as a complementary treatment for people with fibromyalgia, a chronic pain condition that causes widespread muscle tenderness. The vitamins in intravenous vitamin infusions—particularly B-vitamins—are believed to help combat symptoms by providing energy to muscles. Also, some people with fibromyalgia experience low levels of magnesium and other nutrients due to an inability to absorb vitamins; an infusion may provide some immediate nutritional support.

The vitamins in an intravenous vitamin infusion may also help alleviate muscle spasms, pain, and overall fatigue associated with fibromyalgia. Due to these positive effects, some people diagnosed with fibromyalgia avoid using pain medications. The vitamins in a vitamin injection are not addictive, and they will not cause physical dependency or withdrawal symptoms.

Infections

When you get sick with a virus or bacteria, your body’s defense system kicks into high gear. This includes releasing certain proteins (antibodies) that help fight off infection. Antibodies can be created in two ways: one is through eating nutrient-rich foods; another is through intravenous vitamin therapy. When injected into your bloodstream via an IV drip, these vitamins are able to assist your own immune system in fighting off viruses and bacteria.

For example, a vitamin C IV helps fight off a cold by supplying your body with needed nutrients. Though it doesn’t stop you from coming down with an illness, Vitamin C will help your body fight off infections more quickly.

Crohn’s disease

Crohn’s disease is a chronic inflammatory disorder that affects any part of your digestive tract. While there’s no cure for Crohn’s, you can treat symptoms with medication and maintain a healthy diet. Some people find relief by undergoing intravenous therapy to increase hydration or deliver vitamins directly into their bloodstream. This treatment has been shown to improve the quality of life in patients. A balanced diet also supports gut health, as do supplements like vitamins and fish oil. If you’re unable to get these nutrients through your diet, an infusion may be just what you need.

If you’re in search of natural ways to boost your immune system, it can be helpful to make dietary changes or take supplements. Probiotics are one way to deliver beneficial bacteria into your digestive tract. Ingesting certain vitamins is another option that could help alleviate symptoms. Vitamin C, for example, may reduce inflammation in those with Crohn’s disease. Omega-3 fatty acids may also improve symptoms by managing bowel movements, boosting energy levels, and supporting immunity.

Ulcerative colitis

An autoimmune disease of the colon that causes inflammation, abdominal pain, and severe diarrhea. Patients with ulcerative colitis have been shown to be deficient in vitamins needed. These include vitamin B12, folic acid, vitamin D3, and zinc. By supplementing these nutrients, patients can see relief from their symptoms. Infusions can also help restore gut flora by introducing probiotics into a damaged digestive system.

For example, patients with ulcerative colitis may suffer from vitamin B12 deficiency. As a result, they experience fatigue and numbness in their extremities. You can combat these symptoms by treating them with intravenous infusions of vitamin B12.

Getting started

Most clinics that provide intravenous infusions will offer a range of nutrients, including B vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6), C vitamins (C1, C2), and D vitamins (D2), as well as minerals like magnesium. Though most people associate IVs with antibiotics for illnesses like pneumonia or urinary tract infections, these are far from their only use.

In fact, when antibiotics aren’t ideal—either because they aren’t effective or because they pose an increased risk of side effects—IV infusions can be an important alternative to oral antibiotics. They may also help replenish nutrients and electrolytes in people who have lost significant amounts of fluid due to vomiting, diarrhea, or certain medical conditions.
