# Why Is My Urine Yellow After Taking Vitamin B?

Why Do Vitamin B Supplements Turn Urine Yellow?

If you’ve taken a vitamin B supplement recently, you may have noticed some changes to your urine color.

B vitamins are also water-soluble, which means any excess leaves your body through urine. But don’t worry: This side effect is harmless and goes away on its own.

Urine Color and Your Health

In addition to vitamin B intake, a change in urine color might be a sign of dehydration, a urinary tract infection (UTI), or another health condition, says Cleveland Clinic. Some foods and medications may affect urine color, too.

According to Cleveland Clinic, urine is at least 91 percent water. The rest of it contains urea, electrolytes, and other compounds. Urobilin, a by-product of hemoglobin breakdown, gives urine its yellow color.

If your pee is colorless, you may be drinking too much water, which can lead to electrolyte imbalances, says the Nebraska Methodist Health System. But dark yellow urine could be a sign of dehydration.

Light orange or neon yellow urine is usually the result of dietary supplements or medications, such as multivitamins. But it may also indicate liver or bile duct problems, according to Mayo Clinic.

Certain foods, such as carrots, may cause your urine to turn orange, too. If your pee is white or milky, you may have a UTI.

Why Are B Vitamins Important?

There are eight essential B vitamins. Each plays a different role in your body. They help provide your body with energy and create new red blood cells and DNA, says Cleveland Clinic.

Taking B vitamins can also help prevent deficiencies caused by your diet or even certain health conditions.

Low levels of vitamin B12, for instance, can affect your energy and stamina. You may also experience unintentional weight loss, poor appetite, constipation, depression, and memory problems. In the long run, vitamin B12 deficiency may lead to anemia and nervous system damage, according to the NIH.

You’ll want to make sure your diet provides adequate doses of thiamin, or vitamin B1, too, says the NIH. This vitamin helps your body convert food into energy. It’s found in beans, fortified cereals, pork, tuna, brown rice, and other whole foods. Thiamin deficiency has been linked to cardiovascular problems, memory loss, anorexia, and neurological disorders.

The potential health benefits of any B vitamins or B complex vitamins outweigh any potential risks, like bright yellow urine. But before taking any new supplement, it’s wise to speak with your healthcare provider to make sure it’s safe to do so.
