# 'The White Lotus' Finale: People Died But We Were Living

The following story contains spoilers for the finale of The White Lotus Season 2.

Season 1 of The White Lotus had a lot to say about money and class—mainly that those with money tend to remain those with money. It's hard to topple these structures, no matter how many attempts, from how many interested parties, there are to intervene. Season 2 took a closer look at different kinds of relationships, chiefly sexual, but also, at times, romantic and even familial. And the point at the end of Season 2? People live, people learn, and sometimes what you want, what you need, and what is ultimately best all turn out to be entirely different things.

Writer/director Mike White wrapped Season 2 up with a super-sized finale that gave closure for each major storyline in the show—and left the door open for several of them to return in the future, should they decide to take another luxurious and surely drama-free (ha!) trip to a White Lotus resort. And while much of the episode had viewers on edge that our favorite (and favorite-to-hate) characters may not make it out of Sicily alive, it maintained exactly what we love about this show: even when the story is, in theory, stagnant, it's the dialogue, the social situations, and the completely real (and wonderfully-acted) characters that we can't look away from.

There's a lot to talk about with The White Lotus Season 2, and a long time until Season 3. So let's do it, shall we?

Who ended up dying in The White Lotus Season 2?

We were all wondering, we all had theories, but, finally, the question was answered: Tanya (Jennifer Coolidge) was the White Lotus guest who had drowned and was discovered by Daphne. The other bodies discovered, that Rocco told Valentina about in the very first episode, were Quentin (Tom Hollander) and his cohorts Niccolò (Stefano Gianino) Didier (Bruno Gouery) and Hugo (Paolo Camilli) aboard the yacht.

After a call with Portia, Tanya managed to figure out the plot that was unfolding around her—Quentin was in cahoots with her husband, Greg (Jon Gries), in a plot to kill her and take her $500 million fortune. Tanya tried to escape, and when things looked like she had no other choice, she grabbed Niccolò's bag, locked herself in a bedroom, and pulled out his literal Chekhov's gun that we saw last week and started blasting away.

Tanya got herself out of a terrible situation, but instead of waiting to figure out how she could return to the hotel safe and sound, she tried to jump onto the little escape boat that Niccolò had arrived in—and fell, hitting her head on the side of the boat, presumably passing out, and drowning. Oh Tanya.

“I just think her dying at the hands of someone else felt too tragic," White said in an HBO interview that aired after the finale. "It felt like she needed to give her best fight back and that she, in a way had some kind of victory over whoever was conspiring to get rid of her. It just made me laugh to think like she would like take out this cabal of killers and after she successfully does that and she just dies this derpy death and that’s so Tanya.”

Tanya, Greg, Quentin

Greg masterminded the whole situation with Quentin and company, and the signs were there all along. If you return to the early episodes, Tanya says she was "dissociating" but that she saw Greg with "shark eyes" and men with "very effeminate hairstyles" coming for her. Quentin's earliest appearances also make it clear that he always had her in his crosshairs, saying things like "so chic!" just as she walked by, entirely unsolicited.

By the end of the season, you may initially think that Greg gets away with his scheme—Tanya is dead, he gets all they money, and now he doesn't even have to share with Quentin and company.

White also hinted that Greg's story could be explored again in a future season. "I think as far as what happens to Greg, and the conspiracy of Tanya's death, it's possible that Portia is scared enough to just leave it alone," he said. "But the fact that all of those guys die on the boat, feels like there's got to be somebody who's going to track it back down to Greg. But maybe we'll have to wait to find out what happens."

Ethan, Harper, Daphne, and Cameron

Part of what's so wonderful about the way the story between Ethan (Will Sharpe), Harper (Aubrey Plaza), Daphne (Meghann Fahy), and Cameron (Theo James) played out is that outside of a few objective moments—Harper and Cameron taking shots, Harper and Ethan's hotel room door being latched, Cameron touching Harper's leg, Ethan and Daphne walking off to the separate island—we don't really know for sure what happened between these four.

But what we do know is that whatever it was... it seemed to be what they needed. Just like the couple (Connie Britton and Steve Zahn) at the end of Season 1, the experience at the White Lotus, for all of its ups and downs, seemed to bring them closer together.

Harper and Ethan were uncomfortable with their friends, they were uncomfortable with a new social and socioeconomic status, and, as they both started to realize, they had become uncomfortable with each other. So, whatever Harper did with Cameron, and whatever Ethan did with Daphne, it seemed to be for the best. Whatever it was.

Daphne's line to Harper earlier in the season, "Do what you need to do to make yourself feel better" rings through throughout the moment, and especially for her character. She knows that her husband is a cheater, and, as James called him, a 'scumbag.' But perhaps the reason she grew so close to Harper so quickly is that her husband has come onto her friends in the past, ruining those relationships.

In the moment when Ethan tells Daphne his suspicions, that would make sense to be what brought on the range of emotions—disappointment, sadness, anger, recovery—on her face. She's not disappointed in Cameron, she's disappointed in Harper. And she figures out right then and there what she needs to do to feel better about it.

Bert, Dominic, and Albie

It kind of works out for these three, huh? Bert is basically the same surface-level misogynist that he was when we first met him, but through the course of the family's weeklong trip he was faced with some reality: Dominic confronted him at that dinner and told him that he's the way it was. He may not agree, but at least it's something his mind had to briefly wrestle with. And we saw when Lucia was taken away by Massimo (a ploy by Lucia, of course) that Burt really did care about her and seemed concerned. Maybe old dogs can learn new tricks after all. Didn't stop him from making the expected jokes about "Achilles' Cock," but, hey, what are you gonna do.

Albie, expectedly, got played by Lucia. And he guilt tripped his dad into sending him $50,000 to give to Lucia as a "karmic payment." Who didn't see this coming? But maybe it's a lesson that Albie needed to learn. He's a nice kid at his core, but exterior posturing is only getting him so far, and also maybe just be a little cynical for us, Albie. Just once.

Dominic has been the most understated and interesting character in the show. He got a lot of juicy sneaking around sex-scene and threesome moments early on, but the later episodes have focused on him trying to get better; he really does seem to realize that his infidelity has messed his family up, and he wants to get better. Now, is paying his son off to try to fix it the best message? Maybe not. Will it ultimately work? Who knows. But his wife gives him a call near the end of the finale, and it seems like he's going to get his chance.

At the airport, we see Bert, Dom, and Albie all turn their head when a lady walks by; like Bert says, these men are always going to be prisoners to that "Achilles Cock." The key in their lives will be seeing how they react.

Portia and Jack

Predictably, Jack was not a long-term love interest for our friend Portia. Instead, Portia found out that he "was kind of fucking his uncle," as Tanya so eloquently put it, and Jack then basically kidnapped her and brought her directly to the airport, warning her to just get out and stay away from Tanya, Quentin, and whatever was going on with that whole thing.

By the time Portia got to the airport, she found Albie there and the two reflected on what they'd been up to since splitting off. Portia's new man? Deranged. Albie's lady? Yeah, she played him. The two went off looking for something more, and at the end of the day realized: what we had going wasn't so bad after all. The two exchange numbers, setting off the beginning of... a likely uneventful romance of texting every so often and probably not meeting up. But, just like with Albie, Dom, and Bert: the chance is there.

Valentina

Poor Valentina. For much of the season, Valentina played in stark contrast to Season 1's Armond (Murray Bartlett). Valentina did everything right, and by the book; Armond did not, using drugs on the job and having sex with subordinates on the job.

Valentina was so starved when her crush on Isabella turned out to be baseless (Isabella is already engaged to fellow White Lotus employee Rocco) that she couldn't help but accept the kindness of Mia's advances. And this eventually led Valentina to firing Giuseppe, the piano player, and giving Mia the job full time.

It's not even clear if this was Valentina's plan all along but, uh, oh well! If that's her one slip-up during this insane week, then she didn't do too bad. OK, sleeping with a prostitute in one of the hotel rooms she's in charge of isn't great and probably isn't super ethical, but let's give her a pass on this one for now.

By the end of the day, Valentina seems to have come to terms with Isabella and Rocco's relationship, and while she can't pursue a relationship with Mia (because Mia is straight), she's at least got a new friend.

Lucia and Mia

The unquestioned winner of The White Lotus Season 2 is Mia, who fully played father (Dom) and son (Albie) for lots and lots of money. Mia didn't "play" Bert, but his kindness did actually help her with the whole singing thing. It was very nice.

If you didn't notice, the season opens and closes with similar shots of Lucia and Mia walking down the same walkway; "Massimo," whom she told Albie was essentially her pimp that she owed $50,000 to, is present on the walk both of these times. It's clear that she asked him a little favor, and he came through. Dom told Albie he was an easy mark, and he was right.

Lucia wins, Lucia gets lots of money, and we had a great time watching her do it.
