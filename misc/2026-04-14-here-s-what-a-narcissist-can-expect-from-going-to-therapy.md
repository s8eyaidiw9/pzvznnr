# Here's What a Narcissist Can Expect From Going to Therapy

Narcissists typically aren’t the type to do the hard work of self-reflection and exploration required by therapy. (Even though everyone really should.) And when they do, they often turn therapy sessions into another platform to be admired, rather than spending the time needed to really improve their treatment of other people—lessening their sense of entitlement and improving empathy, for example. (You’ve probably seen these tendencies in others, and it’s worth asking how much of them you see in yourself.)

That said, anyone with narcissistic tendencies who genuinely commits to the long and often difficult work of therapy should be commended. For them, what does therapy ultimately look like?

Dr. Ramani explores that question in a new YouTube video, laying out how a good therapist approaches a patient with narcissistic tendencies. As she lays out, despite a wealth of research, “The summary on this is that there’s no slam-dunk therapy for narcissism.” As with so many things, there’s no quick solution, no magic pill. In fact, there isn’t really a standard approach that works with most cases.

What narcissistic clients can expect from therapy, first of all, are limits. That can be especially challenging for people who’ve long felt entitled to no limits. Limits are important to establishing a therapeutic environment, otherwise therapy becomes just an extension of the entitled world narcissists already inhabit.

Within that space, therapists work to establish an open dialogue about emotions and to preserve accountability. Narcissists typically aren’t used to either—and so they tend to get defensive, putting up shields that are an impediment to improving.
