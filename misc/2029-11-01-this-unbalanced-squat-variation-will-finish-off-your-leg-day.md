# This Unbalanced Squat Variation Will Finish Off Your Leg Day

The world is not perfectly balanced—it is round, after all. Not all surfaces are level, especially outside the gym. And because life can sometimes throw out a ton full of rhythm-compromising imbalances, it makes sense every now and then to throw your workout slightly off kilter on purpose to bring some real-world imbalance to your training.

One alternative exercise to add a safe and effective imbalance to your workout is the offset goblet squat, according to Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. This leg day variation, done with one foot slightly elevated while the other remains grounded, intentionally forces you to work through the imbalance, challenging your lower body and your core more than a standard rep.

"This isn't an exercise to go heavy, but it is a squat changeup with real-world carryover," says Samuel. "It's a move that lets you explore your squat a bit, too."

This setup may not necessarily become an everyday move to incorporate into your leg training, but the offset goblet squat is a great way to shake things up as a great finishing move.

How to Do the Offset Goblet Squat

●You'll need a dumbbell (or kettlebell) and a slightly elevated surface for your foot, like a weight plate.

●Hold the weight in the goblet position with both hands, then place one foot on the elevated surface. Your other foot should be on the ground, at hip-width or at your typical squat position.

●Squeeze your glutes, abs, and shoulder blades to create tension and prevent the dumbbell from tipping you forward. Keep your weight in your heels.

●Then perform this move like any other squat—lower down to a comfortable depth. The asymmetrical elevation of your leg will force you to drop deeper in your squat stance on that elevated side, emphasizing the glute stretch. After a quick beat, drive back up, squeezing your glutes.

You won't break any weight records with this squat variation, but you should keep the reps high. Start with three sets of 12 to 15 reps to challenge your squats in a different way.
