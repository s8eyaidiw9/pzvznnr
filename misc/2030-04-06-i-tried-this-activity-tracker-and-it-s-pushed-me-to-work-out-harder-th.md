Previously, I’d already been tracking my runs with the Garmin Forerunner 10, but I was looking for something I could take to the gym, too. Plus, I was also hoping for a fitness tracker that could give a clearer picture of how many calories I was actually burning during my workout. The Forerunner 10 gave only a barebones guesstimate, based only on height, weight, and your run—it didn’t measure heart rate, so it couldn’t take intensity into account.

It syncs with your phone by Bluetooth, which allows you to get smart notifications for calls, texts, and e-mails—yep, you can even read them from your wrist—as well as control your music from your wrist, too.

My Garmin Vivosmart HR+ has been my loyal workout companion every day since then, and I’ve found its heart rate monitor especially helpful when running—particularly on interval days when I felt like death during the sprints. Seeing tangible proof that you’re working harder than usual was a big motivator to me, and gave me the push I needed to continue on with the workout.

Another plus with running? The Garmin’s floors-climbed sensor must take elevation into account, because it counts rolling hills toward your goal. Just one, 30-minute session of hill intervals can allow me to reach my goal of 10 floors per day, which is pretty awesome to check off your list before noon hits.

The Garmin was great while running, but the real test would come in the weight room, which took up a bigger chunk of my weekly workout hours. There’s no set “activity” for lifting, but its “other” function served the purpose well enough. With the watch, I was able to accurately time my rest periods for the first time, as well as have a real-time view of my heart rate and calories burned.

And that was a workout motivator itself—I found that if I noticed my heart rate remaining high, I’d be more likely to tack on another exercise or two at the end to take advantage of the calorie burn. The watch also logs your workout intensity per week, or how many minutes you've spent above a certain heart rate, so I’ve been challenging myself to meet or beat my previous amount each week, too.

Now, that’s not to say the fitness tracker was perfect, though. It had a few…interesting idiosyncrasies. For one, the heart rate monitor would go wonky during some strength training sessions, mostly on back or shoulder days, when your arms would go above your head. On those days, it would be common for my heart rate to drop as low as the 40s—well below my resting heart rate in the low 60s—even when I’d be dripping with sweat during a superset.

Also, the watch was super snappy at picking up a GPS signal in the beginning, but it began to take longer and longer. Now, with the temperature getting colder, I don’t want to stand around waiting for it to pick up—which has been taking longer than five minutes. There’s a simple workaround, though: I just set it to pick up GPS, leave it outside, and then get dressed for my run. By the time I’m ready, the watch is, too.

But probably the weirdest issue? The watch randomly triggers my phone to play music whenever I’m in the shower. Not a huge deal when I’m in my apartment by myself, but it did lead to some raised eyebrows when I inadvertently blasted “I Will Survive” in our company locker room first thing in the morning.

None of those glitches are deal-breakers, though. While the Garmin Vívosmart HR+ doesn’t give me an exact replica of what I’m doing each and every workout, it does spit out accurate enough data that I can use to track a general pattern of my fitness. And isn’t that its job in the first place?
