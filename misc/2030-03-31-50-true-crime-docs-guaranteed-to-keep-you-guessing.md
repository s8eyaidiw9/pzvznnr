# 50 True Crime Docs Guaranteed to Keep You Guessing

In 2023, true crime is everywhere. What was once a niche interest has blown up into a massive, bankable business. Sometimes well-considered, sometimes simply lurid, these true crime stories exist as both prestige content and filler on the big screen, actual TV, and—where they especially thrive—the streaming giants such as Netflix, Hulu, HBO Max, etc.

[Keep You](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/7627429051.html)

But all that content sometimes feels like a bit too much to tackle. It can be difficult for all but the dedicated true crime head (hey, we’ve been here for decades, binging Forensic Files marathons) to figure out which are the truly worthwhile, thoughtful, and inquisitive titles in the surge of nonfiction crime documentaries.

That’s where we come in. We’ve spent numerous hours recalling, cataloging, and researching hundreds of true crime documentary movies and series released over the past several decades. And we ultimately whittled down our list of the best offerings out there. The grizzly glories appropriately stretch back to Errol Morris’s 1988 masterpiece The Thin Blue Line, which blew up the genre and had real legal impact with its shocking conclusion. Our list may not exactly be scientific, but in its subjectivity, it has been rigorously mulled over.

The criteria for our true crime documentary highlights were simple: These excellent films and shows needed to be more than enticing diversions; they should add a depth of narrative, complexity of ideas, and/or pure cinematic propulsion to the mix. We landed on everything from British documentarian Nick Broomfield’s phenomenal run of scandal-sorting documentaries to some favorites on Netflix and HBO Max, like Wild Wild Country and The Jinx, respectively.

Here are the 50 essential true crime documentaries, in alphabetical order, deserving of your attention.

Aileen: Life and Death of a Serial Killer (2003)

Errol Morris might be the OG of great true crime documentary pulp, but British director Nick Broomfield is the prolific master of the genre, hence why he has the most titles here (five). His movies often center himself as an amusingly nosey reporter, but then extend outward in mind-bending, indelibly rich explorations of seemingly tabloidy subjects. Aileen Wuronous long ago got tagged as “the” female serial killer, but Broomfield in his second movie about the convicted murderer empathetically and devastatingly takes in an older, worn-down Wuornos’s mental struggles and reckoning with the past as she approaches her execution date.

All the Beauty and Bloodshed (2022)

The most recent entry here, Laura Poitras’s (Citizenfour) movie weaves together the wrenching story of photographer Nan Goldin’s career and the opioid addiction that animated her activism against powerful figures peddling the prescription drugs, particularly the Sackler family and its Purdue Pharma, which have been forced to pay billions in civil judgments for their central role in the opioid crisis, even if they have avoided criminal penalties. The emotionless evil underlying pharmaceutical companies’ actions warrants inclusion here.

Amanda Knox

[Keep You](https://metabolic-horizon.bbsxyxy.com/blog/9226554904.html)

This Netflix doc benefits from the years-later access to Knox herself, the largely unheard but widely-seen young, beautiful alleged seductress at the center of a globally covered criminal trial in Italy. Her account feels honest. Rather than emerging victorious, she mostly comes off as defeated.

Baby God (2020)

A succinct, disturbing look at the infamous fertility doctor Dr. Quincy Fortier, who secretly inseminated his patients, and his kids’ ensuing search for identity.

Biggie & Tupac (2002)

Broomfield is best out of his element. Here he trains his lens, and fearless investigative spirit (the Suge Knight interview is nerve-rattling), on the beautiful lives, art, and unsolved murders of the hip-hop legends.

Capturing the Friedmans (2003)

Before he directed The Jinx, Andrew Jarecki shocked the arthouse crowd with his 2003 Academy Award-nominated film unearthing a family history poisoned by secrets and charges of child sexual abuse.

The Case Against Adnan Syed (2019)

Didn’t catch the first season of the Serial podcast covering this did-he-or-didn’t-he mystery? No problem. This HBO miniseries concisely sums up the salient facts and intrigue.

Casting JonBenet (2018)

Heard enough about the tragic 1996 killing of child beauty queen JonBenet Ramsey—the credible theories, the crackpot conspiracy theories, and everything in between? This 80-minute doc finds strange, haunting new meaning in the case by attempting to cast, and interviewing, Colorado locals in re-enactments of the Ramsey saga that become communal catharsis.

The Central Park Five (2012)

Before you check out Ava DuVernay’s fictionalized When They See Us, dig into Ken Burns’s definitive PBS documentary on the racially incendiary New York City case that was tried as much in the media as in court.

Cocaine Cowboys (2006)

[You Guessing](https://beautybiometrics.hbsnzj.com/)

Miami true crime doesn’t get more enjoyably sleazy than this 2006 film exploring the real-life characters behind the neon-lit beach city’s ‘80s drug trade.

Cropsey (2009)

A tight, small-budget film that justly achieved cult status on streaming, Cropsey teases out Staten Island urban legend before plunging into the real sinister child kidnappings that rocked the borough in the ‘70s and ‘80s.

The Devil Next Door (2020)

Ever wonder if your neighbor is maybe a secret murderous Nazi? The Devil Next Door’s tortured mystery, about an Ohio man suspected of a horrific past as a death camp guard and the slippery evidence in question, has you covered.

Frank Serpico (2017)

This 2017 film revisits the Al Pacino classic-inspiring recluse, and former NYPD officer and whistleblower within the department during the ‘60s and ‘70s, finding a man just as grizzled and impressive as you’d expect.

Free Meek (2020)

The Jay-Z-produced miniseries does a captivating job of locating the bigger picture of criminal justice reform tied to the campaign to “Free Meek,” aka rapper Meek Mill.

Fyre: The Greatest Party That Never Happened (2019)

Of the two competing Fyre Festival streaming documentaries, this Netflix entry has the rawest up-close inspection of the unholy carnival of cons.

Gringo: The Dangerous Life of John McAfee (2017)

John McAfee hated this riveting documentary from Oscar-nominated filmmaker Nanette Burstein. It’s not hard to see why: As it clearly shows without telling, the late tech mogul was a deranged narcissist, along with being a possible murderer who fled his Belize compound, leaving more bonkers questions than firm answers in his wake.

Heidi Fleiss: Hollywood Madam (1995)

Nick Broomfield rolls around in the muck of L.A.’s criminal underbelly, coming away with a viscerally enthralling portrait of Fleiss, escort procurer to the stars, and her downfall. As Roger Ebert wrote, “We hear several versions of the same stories. Someone is lying, yes—but is anyone telling the truth?”

I Love You, Now Die (2019)

While it can’t quite live up to the amazing title, I Love You, Now Die intelligently guides the viewer through a knotty texting suicide case that raises difficult questions about our plugged-in lives and its escalating dangers for youth.

Icarus (2017)

Best Documentary Oscar winner for good reason, Icarus finds director Bryan Fogel unexpectedly sinking into the web of a massive international doping scandal.
