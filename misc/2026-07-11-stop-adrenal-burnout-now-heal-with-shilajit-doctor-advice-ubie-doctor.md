Understanding Adrenal Stress and Why Recovery Matters

Your adrenal glands sit atop your kidneys and produce hormones like cortisol, adrenaline, and DHEA. These hormones help you handle stress, regulate energy, and balance blood sugar. When stress is chronic—whether from work, poor sleep, inflammation, or emotional strain—the adrenals can become overworked. Over time, you might experience:

Persistent fatigue or "wired but tired" feeling

Difficulty focusing or "brain fog"

Mood swings, irritability, or anxiety

Sugar cravings, weight gain around the midsection

Sleep disturbances or insomnia

Improving adrenal function isn't just about reducing symptoms. It's about restoring resilience so your body can adapt to life's highs and lows.

What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like substance found in Himalayan and other high-altitude rock formations. Used for centuries in Ayurvedic medicine, it's prized for its adaptogenic and rejuvenating properties. Key points:

Contains fulvic acid, humic acid, trace minerals, and antioxidants

Believed to support energy production at the cellular level

May help balance hormones, including cortisol and DHEA

Clinically studied for anti-inflammatory and anti-aging effects

By helping your body adapt to stress and supporting mitochondrial health, Shilajit can play an important role in adrenal stress recovery.

Practical Steps for Adrenal Recovery with Shilajit

Consult a Healthcare ProfessionalBefore starting any supplement—especially if you're on medications or have chronic conditions—talk to your doctor or a qualified naturopath.

Choose High-Quality Shilajit

Look for standardized extracts with ≥ 50% fulvic acid.

Ensure third-party testing for heavy metals and contaminants.

Prefer resin form or water-soluble powder over low-quality capsules.

Start with a Low Dose and Titrate

Typical starting dose: 100–200 mg daily, in the morning.

Observe energy levels, sleep quality, and mood for 1–2 weeks.

Gradually increase up to 300–500 mg/day if tolerated.

Timing Matters

Take Shilajit in the morning or early afternoon with water or milk.

Avoid late-day dosing, as adaptogens can be mildly stimulating.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Supplements can be powerful, but they're not a substitute for professional care when it's needed. Contact your doctor if you experience:

Unexplained weight loss or gain

Severe insomnia or sleep apnea

Chest pain, palpitations, or irregular heartbeat

Fainting, dizziness, or sudden blood pressure changes

Intense anxiety, depression, or thoughts of self-harm

Always inform your healthcare provider about any herbs or adaptogens you're taking to avoid interactions with medications.
