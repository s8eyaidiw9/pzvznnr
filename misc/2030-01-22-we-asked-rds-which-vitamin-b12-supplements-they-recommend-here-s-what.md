When to Consult Your Doctor

Vitamin and mineral supplements can help fill nutritional gaps in your diet, but if they’re not taken correctly, they can cause harm. That’s why it’s recommended that you talk to a healthcare provider before you start taking any new vitamin or supplement.

It’s also important to note that dietary supplements are not regulated by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Be sure to look for third-party testing and certifications on your supplement, as this guarantees quality, purity, and accurate label claims. Third-party certifications to look out for include NSF International, USP, ConsumerLab, and Informed Choice.

What to Look For When Buying B12 Supplements

With the abundance of B12 supplements currently on the market, there are a few factors to consider when shopping for the right one.

Third-party testing: This ensures the product meets safety, potency, and purity standards, so you know exactly what you're putting into your body. Organizations that offer third-party testing include:

NSF International is a product testing, inspection, and certification organization for public health standards.

NSF for Sport is an independent third-party certification program helping athletes make safer decisions when choosing sports supplements.

USP is an independent scientific nonprofit organization that aims to help protect patient safety in the world's medicines, dietary supplements, and foods.

ConsumerLab evaluates consumer products relating to health, wellness, and nutrition, providing independent test results and information to help consumers and healthcare professionals identify the best products.

Informed Choice is a global quality assurance and third-party supplement testing program designed to minimize the risks of dietary supplement products from contamination with prohibited and potentially harmful substances.

[17]

The presence of artificial additives such as flavors, colors, and fillers: Simply put, the fewer, the better.

Price: Be sure to do the math. While some price points may not be much, the supplement may only have a 30-day supply. Be sure to check the dosage, total servings, and servings per container and then compare prices.

Form of the supplement: Whether it’s a spray, chewable, tablet, or liquid, choose a format you’re comfortable with — especially if you don’t like swallowing pills.

It fits your lifestyle: If you are vegan or vegetarian, be sure to find products that are, in fact, certified vegan and without animal products. If you forget to take supplements, do not opt for one that requires multiple daily doses to meet your needs. Find a one-per-day option.

Limited or no sugar: Sugar is added to improve taste in some supplements. As sugar can increase your risk of tooth decay, it’s advisable to find a supplement with limited or no added sugar if possible.
