# Try This 5-Move Warmup Before Your Next Bike Ride

We know it’s tempting to just hop on your bike and get pedaling, but a quick flow to get your energy up and heart pumping can help you boost your performance on your next bike ride or stationary spin session.

In our latest episode of The Fix, Daniel Giordano, DPT, PT, CSCS of Bespoke Treatments, breaks down exactly what to do for your pre-cycling warmup. Read the summary of the routine below, and watch the video for a play-by-play tutorial of each move.

The Best Bike Riding Warmup

High Knee Pull to Forward Lunge

This first stretch helps your lower body get ready to ride. Pull one knee up to your chest and raise up to your toes on the opposite leg. Immediately step forward into a lunge. Step back and reset. “You want to put a little mechanical stress on that knee,” says Giordano. Do five to eight repetitions or 30 seconds on each side to get your quads, knees, and hips fired up for your workout.

Cradle Pull

Onto the second active stretch. Standing up, grab your knee with one hand and put your other hand on your ankle so you can pull upwards to stretch the back of your glutes and your hips. Do five to eight reps on each side or 30 seconds total on your right and left legs. “If your range of motion is not there, you're gonna have restrictions through your joints,” says Giordano. So let's make sure that these active stretches are increasing that range of motion for those joints, so those joints are moving better, and there’s less pressure on that joint and more freedom to move during your cycle.”

Hamstring Scoop to Reverse Lunge

Step one foot in front of the other, bending your knee and keeping the front leg straight. Reach down with both arms and "scoop" forward and back up. Immediately step the front leg back into a reverse lunge. You’re stretching the straight leg side by pushing through the reverse lunge and pushing through the front foot to get your hips and posterior chain active. “You want to make sure that our posterior chain is ready for activity as we cycle. If we don’t get that posterior chain ready, you're not gonna get that power you need to increase your wattage during your cycle,” says Giordano. Do five to eight repetitions or 30 seconds on each side.

Quad Pull to Lateral Line Stretch

Grab your ankle with your hand and pull it back as you feel the stretch through your quad. Then drop it down and then reach over and up with the same arm, creating the stretch for the anterior aspect of the hips. This will help to get the hip flexor and the psoas muscle actively stretched and prepped for activity. “We need to make sure as cyclists that our hip flexors have the range of motion, and the activation, so that you can drive through as you go through our clock on our cycling,” says Giordano. Do five to eight of these each side or 30 seconds on each side so you get that anterior chain raring to go for your next bike ride.

1 ¼ Squat

Drop to a squat, with your thighs parallel with the ground (or slightly lower), then rise a quarter of the way back up, pause, go back to parallel, then rise all the way back up. “We're doing these to get your legs and the power in your legs to start being active and ready for that ride,” says Giordano. “We need to make sure that we have the range of motion for our cycling, but also that our legs are ready to produce that power and that wattage to perform at the optimal level on your next cycling experience.” Do one-and-a-quarter reps five to eight times or 30 seconds on each side for these to activate.

Yes, doing these five quick moves will pay off, folks. “Make sure you're spending a couple of minutes prior to your next ride so that you can warm up your body, reduce your risk of injury, and produce the most power and wattage so that you can crush that ride,” says Giordano.
