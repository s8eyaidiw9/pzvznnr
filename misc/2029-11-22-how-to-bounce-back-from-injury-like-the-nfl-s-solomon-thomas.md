# How to Bounce Back From Injury Like the NFL's Solomon Thomas

[Like the](https://7161777.com/ylraa24/)

Before 2020, Solomon Thomas had never had surgery. But in February 2020, the then 49ers defensive lineman tore his labrum. About a month later, he underwent surgery for a bone spur in his ankle. He managed to recover and start the 2020 season. But after only two games, Thomas tore his ACL, ending his season. Adding injury to injury? His fourth surgery, in December 2020, for yet another bone spur. “I was healthy for maybe three or four weeks—total—in 2020,” Thomas, 26, says. “It was a long year.”

Not just for his body, either. To get ready for the 2021 season, Thomas, who’s now with the Las Vegas Raiders, had to rebuild his mind, learning to trust a body that had betrayed him. Here’s Thomas’s five-step process for forging the grit to endure psychologically.

1. ACKNOWLEDGE THE PAIN

Thomas tore his ACL onSeptember 20 against the Jets and underwent surgery two weeks later. Days afterward, he had a good cry. “I started bawling,” he says. “I was like, ‘I’m so sick of being in pain.’ I couldn’t see a light at the end of the tunnel.” The cry helped him feel better mentally, and for good reason: Research has shown crying can help alleviate perceptions of pain.

2. DO MENTAL MUSCLE REPS

Thomas began his rehab after two weeks, getting physical therapy and working to regain range of motion in his knee.He also made time daily to improve his mental state, doing guided meditations from his mental coach, Graham Betchart. (He used the Headspace app, too.) “The meditations,” he says, “are a great reset. [I remind myself] ‘This is a temporary feeling.’”

3. JOURNAL YOUR PROGRESS AND SETBACKS

It’s easy to see physical progress during rehab, but Thomas needed to see mental progress as well. So he began journaling once a day. He’d start each morning by recording daily affirmations, then he wrote down how he was feeling (good, bad, indifferent).

4. RECOVER ATA GENTLE PACE

[Bounce Back](https://ppmaas.com/mx5akz/)

By December, Thomas was ahead of schedule, squatting with weight. Then he felt a pinch in his ankle. He had another bone spur. After surgery to correct that, he returned to training—with a slower mentality. “A key is balance,” he says. “You have to be intentional about having that reset and energy to focus again.”

5. RESPECT YOUR TIMELINE

“You can’t expect the process to be perfect; it’s six to nine months,”Thomas says. He understood that after his second bone spur: Even if you’re ahead of schedule, you must expect hiccups. “It’s a grind,” he says.
