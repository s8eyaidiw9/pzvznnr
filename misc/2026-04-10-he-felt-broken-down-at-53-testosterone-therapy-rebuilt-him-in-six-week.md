# He Felt Broken Down At 53. Testosterone Therapy Rebuilt Him In Six Weeks.

Is it taking you a little longer to recover from workouts? Maybe you’re also noticing some shifts in your sleep quality, sex drive, or mental clarity? It might not be “just stress” or even what you’re eating or drinking. It could be due to normal age-related hormonal changes—a biological shift that can affect our mood, memory, sleep, sex lives, muscles, bones—basically every part of who we are.

But just because it’s “normal” doesn’t mean you have to accept feeling subpar. In the third episode of The Future You, we dive into how you can identify what's happening in your body and thrive with all the changes.

Host and editorial director of Men’s and Women’s Health Rich Dorment sits down with Hollywood action star Frank Grillo (who's currently starring in Peacemaker and Tulsa King), best-selling author Mel Robbins, and Dr. Priya Jaisinghani from NYU Langone Health for an insightful conversation around perimenopause, menopause, andropause, and much more.

Here’s a look at some of the themes covered in the episode:

The critical role of both testosterone and estrogen in the body

Testosterone is known as the primary male sex hormone and estrogen is known as the primary female sex hormone, but everybody has both. The two hormones play a role in your mood and motivation as well as in hair, skin, muscle, and bone health.

Grillo’s experience with low testosterone

At around 53, Grillo, now 60, started to feel joint pain and was struggling to get a good night’s sleep and to feel fully recovered after workouts. After blood tests revealed that he had low testosterone, he was prescribed the hormone and noticed an "extraordinary" change within about six weeks.

When men may experience andropause

Testosterone levels in some men can start decreasing as early as their mid-twenties due to factors unrelated to aging. With advancing age, after 30 there's up to a 1% gradual decline per year, Dr. Jaisinghani says, adding that as many as one in three men over the age of 45 have low testosterone on their labs. It can start out really subtle—maybe mood changes or fatigue—but symptoms can build over time, including breast enlargement, loss of body hair, and/or a decreased sex drive.

The stigma around low testosterone

[Rebuilt Him In](https://immunopeptide.xianfengchem.com/blog/4875898718.html)

Dorment and Grillo talk about why testosterone therapy is still somewhat taboo and Dr. Jaisinghani says it’s important to have these conversations so that others will get the courage to go see their doctor. It's not just about vanity, it's also about your health, and if you have a treatable hormonal condition, you should absolutely treat it.

How body composition can be affected by hormones

Aging and hormonal changes can predispose both men and women to metabolic disorders like insulin resistance, diabetes and high cholesterol. Loss of muscle mass also comes with age, which makes it critical to lift weights and make sure you’re getting adequate protein and fiber.

Why a comprehensive approach is key

When you’re tested for testosterone, you’ll actually have a comprehensive panel that includes other hormones like prolactin and estrogen, which can also impact mood, motivation, energy, bone density, and more. “You need to have the whole ecosystem, the whole context, to understand the path forward,” Dr. Jaisinghani says.

What's considered normal when it comes to testosterone

Dr. Jaisinghani says that a “normal” range of testosterone is about 300 to 1000 ng/dL, which is a wide range. However, whether or not you’d benefit from testosterone therapy is not just about the number, but also your personal symptoms.

The innovations in testosterone replacement therapy

Dr. Jaisinghani says there are now injectables that require less frequency of dosing. There are also patches, nasal gels, and more, and it’s important to work with your provider to determine the best approach for you.

Why men should be talking about menopause

Robbins says menopause affects everybody. “We want men and boys and everyone to understand that 100% of the women in your life are going to go through a life-changing medical condition that they cannot avoid," she says. "I'd like all men to be involved in this because it impacts every single woman that you care about.”
