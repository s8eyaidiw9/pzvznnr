What to Consider

Inseam

5-inch inseam

This is above mid-thigh territory. It’s athletic, retro, and definitely makes a statement. Think ‘70s running shorts meet ‘90s skater, but in denim. Not for everyone, but if you’ve got the legs and/or the confidence, it’s a power move.

6-inch inseam

This lands a bit safer, usually a few inches above the knee. It still has that throwback energy, but it’s more wearable for everyday guys who want a slimmer, higher-cut look without going full short-short.

7- to 9-inseam

This is the sweet spot for most guys. Either it hits above or at the knee, without looking too long or too short.

Fit

Relaxed Fit

This is your go-to if you want comfort and a little extra room. Relaxed fits are cut wider through the thigh and seat, often paired with a longer inseam (think 8–10 inches). They give off a chill, easygoing vibe that’s perfect for guys who don’t want anything clinging to their legs.

Loose Fit

Loose is like relaxed, but turned up a notch—think baggy, intentionally oversized, and often cut longer or with more exaggerated proportions. It leans more fashion-forward or retro. Guys chasing that Y2K or streetwear aesthetic, or anyone who prioritizes comfort over tailoring.

Straight Fit

This one’s the most balanced: not too tight, not too wide. Straight fits go from hip to knee with a clean line, offering structure without clinging. It’s the most versatile option—think “classic” or “standard.”

Slim Fit

Slim fits taper slightly through the thigh and leg, hugging the body without being skinny. They give a more tailored, modern look, but they’re not for everyone, especially in non-stretch denim.

Fabric

Most jean shorts are made from 100 percent cotton for that sturdy, vintage feel, while others mix in a bit of spandex or elastane for stretch and comfort. If you plan on moving, sitting, or walking around all day, that bit of stretch can make a big difference. For a raw denim or more rugged feel, go all-cotton—but know it’ll break in over time.

Wash and Finish

The wash sets the tone. Medium blue is the most versatile. It’s casual but still polished. Light wash or stone wash gives more laid-back, vintage energy. Black or dark denim can skew dressier or a bit edgier. Look for distressing, patchwork, or frayed hems if you want a statement piece. Clean hems feel more elevated.

What Exactly Are Jorts?

Jean shorts, abbreviated to jorts, are just that. Any kind of shorts that are made out of denim. Full length jeans can be cut down into a short inseam, often leaving a desired fraying, or the garment can be short to begin with. The variation in jean shorts designs are just as wide ranging as chinos or any other material used to make shorts. The only reason that jorts are so often separated into their own category is an infamous fashion history.

How to Style Jean Shorts

When it comes to styling jean shorts, you have to consider how baggy and long you want them to be. There are essentially two schools of thought on jorts: The retro look that drops below the knee, or close to, and runs baggy. This often includes cargo and carpenter shorts. Complete the retro look with oversized t-shirts, button downs, and light jackets. High ankle socks with sneakers are a good way to complete the look.

The other way to style jean shorts is to simply think of them as the shorts you’re used to, just in a different fabric. This applies to any jean shorts that fit common contemporary short criteria of inseams running from 5 to 8 inches, slim fitting, and featuring a standard five pocket design. This route offers a wide range of styling because you can pretty much wear them with anything in your wardrobe, from aloha shirts to oxfords. Complete the look with loafers or sandals.
