Explanation

Nasal irrigation with a neti pot is a simple, drug-free way to gently flush mucus, allergens and irritants from your nasal passages. When done properly, it can:

Relieve nasal congestion

Reduce post-nasal drip

Improve breathing and sense of smell

Support recovery after sinus surgery

Below is a thorough, step-by-step guide—backed by credible medical sources—to help you learn how to use a neti pot correctly, including special tips if you're preparing for sinus surgery. Always discuss any serious or life-threatening concerns with your doctor.

1. Why Nasal Irrigation Works

Science shows that rinsing the nasal cavity:

Thins mucus and helps it drain

Removes pollen, dust, bacteria and viruses

Reduces inflammation of the nasal lining

May decrease the need for medications in chronic sinusitis

A 2018 Cochrane review found nasal irrigation safe and effective for long-term management of chronic rhinosinusitis.

2. Precautions and Safety First

Before you begin:

Always use sterile, distilled, or previously boiled water. Tap water may contain organisms that can cause rare but serious infections.

4. Step-by-Step Rinsing Technique

Prepare the saline solution• Mix salt and baking soda into warm, sterile water until fully dissolved.• Pour into the neti pot.

Position yourself• Lean over a sink.• Turn your head 45 degrees to one side.

Insert and pour• Gently place the spout into the upper nostril to form a seal.• Breathe through your mouth.• Tip the neti pot so saline flows into the upper nostril, draining out the lower nostril.

Switch sides• Blow your nose gently to clear remaining saline and mucus.• Refill the neti pot if needed.• Tilt your head the other way and repeat.

Finish up• Gently blow your nose again.• Clean and dry your neti pot.

Total rinse time: about 2–3 minutes. Aim for once or twice daily during flare-ups, or as directed by your doctor.

5. Tips for Effective Rinsing

Use tepid (body temperature) water to avoid discomfort.

Keep your head steady—no jerking or sudden movements.

Don't sniff or swallow the saline; let gravity do the work.

If you feel stinging, your solution may be too salty or not well dissolved—adjust the mix.

Replace your neti pot periodically and disinfect if you notice any film or deposits.

6. Sinus Surgery Prep: Why and How

If you're preparing for sinus surgery, nasal irrigation can:

Reduce swelling and improve surgical visibility

Lower bacterial load in your sinuses

Speed post-operative healing

For sinus surgery prep:

Start rinsing 1–2 weeks before your scheduled date, once daily.

Increase to twice daily in the 48 hours before surgery, unless your surgeon advises otherwise.

Use a preservative-free saline recommended by your ENT specialist.

Report any unusual pain, bleeding or severe congestion to your surgeon promptly.

Always follow your surgeon's specific instructions on irrigation timing and solution concentration.

7. Aftercare and Maintenance

Continue irrigation as advised—often twice a day for the first week after surgery, then taper.

Monitor for signs of infection: fever, foul-smelling discharge or increased facial pain.

Keep follow-up appointments; your ENT may suction your sinuses to remove crusts and debris.

Practice good hygiene: wash hands before and after irrigation, and avoid sharing your neti pot.

8. When to Seek Professional Advice

Nasal irrigation is generally safe, but consult your doctor if you experience:

Severe or persistent pain

Prolonged nosebleeds

Signs of sinus or ear infection (fever, swelling, intense headache)

No improvement after a week of regular rinsing
