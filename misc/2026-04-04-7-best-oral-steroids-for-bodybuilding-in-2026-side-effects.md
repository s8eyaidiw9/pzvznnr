Side Effects of Oral Steroids

Compared to their injectable versions, oral steroids tend to be on the steeper side in terms of pricing; moreover, their side effects can be a bit more unique apart from the standard ones associated with all steroids.

Let’s take a look:

Oral Steroids are almost always liver toxic

Barring Oxandrolone, pill steroids will always place a strain on the liver. While some are less intense than others, it is still important to keep in mind the potential harm that could be done to your body.

Primarily, oral steroids are toxic because of their metabolism and chemical structure.

An excessively long oral steroid cycle can impair the flow of bile from the liver which can lead to cholestasis – the buildup of bile can result in liver damage.

Oxidative stress caused by steroid metabolism can contribute to liver toxicity by damaging liver cells.

This kind of hepatic strain can be caused by milder steroids like Test as well, therefore it is imperative for people using testosterone oral steroids – or any other – to monitor their liver health closely.

Increased Water Retention

Oral steroids use water retention to bring a bit of fullness into the body by drawing water into the cells.

However, due to this water-retaining tendency, you must regularly monitor your blood pressure and kidney function to ensure you do not run into serious problems during your oral steroid cycle.

Cardiovascular Strain

Prolonged use of anabolic steroids can put your cardiovascular health at risk. Steroids can raise the level of LDL cholesterol and bad cholesterol, which can lead to plaque buildup in the arteries.

An increase in blood pressure can be expected due to its strong androgenic effects. This combined with changes in the lipid profile can significantly increase the risk of a stroke or heart attack.

Amongst a lot of steroids, Trenbolone and Anadrol – the best bulking oral steroids – are infamous for causing the most cardiovascular-associated problems.

Psychiatric Issues

Oral anabolic steroids can cause users to experience rapid changes in mood, these may include aggression, depression, and irritability – this is often referred to as ‘roid rage.’
