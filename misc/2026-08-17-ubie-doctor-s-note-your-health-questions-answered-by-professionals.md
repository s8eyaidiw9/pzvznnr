POTS in Women: Managing Dizziness and Heart Rate Spikes

POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome) most often affects women ages 15 to 50, causing dizziness, lightheadedness, and a rapid heart rate upon standing. It's diagnosed when standing triggers a heart rate increase of at least 30 bpm without a significant drop in blood pressure, reflecting autonomic nervous system dysfunction. Management typically includes increased fluids and salt, compression garments, slow position changes, heat avoidance, and graded recumbent exercise. Medications and evaluation for overlapping conditions may be added when symptoms persist.
