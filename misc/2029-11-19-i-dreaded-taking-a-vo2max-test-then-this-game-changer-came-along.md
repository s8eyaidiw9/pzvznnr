# I Dreaded Taking a VO2max Test. Then This Game Changer Came Along.

FOR ABOUT A a year, an exercise physiologist I know kept offering to do a VO2 max test on me. For more than a year, I’d been resisting it. Not because it’s not important. Plenty of trends have come and gone in my decades as a health editor, but VO2 max has been a nonstop part of the fitness conversation since before I even got in the game.

As a coach and competitive triathlete, I knew that high numbers are generally linked to high performance—the test quantifies the size of your aerobic engine. But now it’s having a serious moment in the spotlight because VO2 max has been unquestionably linked to a longer life (and it can even tell you something about how your heart is aging).

As an endurance athlete, endure is what I do for 6, 8, 10 hours in events like half-Ironmans and 28-mile swims. But the claustrophobia I felt during a 10-minute VO2 max test about two years ago put me off getting another, even though I’d managed to misplace the results. Plus, they’d only be marginally useful now, since VO2 max changes with the training you’re doing (or not doing).

But I’d been noticing that I wasn’t putting out as many watts on the bike as usual and wasn’t hitting my splits in the pool, even though I wasn’t just phoning in my workouts. What if my VO2 max was lower than it should be for my age? What if the training I’d been doing to raise it wasn’t tough enough?

[This Game Changer](https://ageless-peptides.china-akan.com/blog/6461186317.html)

I dreaded the test. It wasn’t the max effort I worried about. It was the apparatus.

VO2 is measured by tracking what you’re inhaling and exhaling as you ramp up your effort all the way on a treadmill or bike. That means the people running the test need to make sure no gases escape. Last time I got it done, that meant my nose was blocked with a nose clip while a mask attached to a hose covered my mouth, which is pretty unnerving, especially if you don’t like feeling as if you’re suffocating. My heart rate was a visible chronicle of my panic.

But about a month ago, I signed up to beta test a new high-performance and health care company called Eternal. I was fully aware that in order to get a solid picture of my health, they needed to get my VO2 max, so I caved. The day came. The technicians got everything rigged up, and…

[Taking VO max Test](https://3899241.com/hcjgvq/)

Hey, wait! I could breathe! They’d put me into the game-changing VO2 Master, a portable collection device that has a flexible, comfortable mask that covers both your nose and mouth. Without the claustrophobia, you can just focus on giving your all to the test. That type of mask is pretty available for VO2 max tests now (it was originally designed for anesthesia). And that’s a good thing, because this test is one more access road to knowing more than ever about how our bodies are doing.

[max Test](https://cellular.wiki/4taujmaqq/)

For me, the experience didn’t just give me my VO2 max. It reminded me that dread isn’t a terribly useful health strategy. Committing to the test pointed out that tech changes fast, and old information can leave you stuck.

And with VO2 max, you don’t have to fear the result—you can move that number upward with short efforts that push you, even if it’s in the basement to start with. (This workout is one good way to boost it.) Yet VO2 max isn’t everything. Mine ended up showing me that the high-intensity training I’ve been doing has been working after all. But it also means I need to look elsewhere—efficiency, recovery, nutrition, or a myriad of other factors—if I’m going to climb my way up the race results. But of course, I’ll keep the VO2 training too. And if I wonder how well I’m doing on it? At least I won’t dread the test anymore.
