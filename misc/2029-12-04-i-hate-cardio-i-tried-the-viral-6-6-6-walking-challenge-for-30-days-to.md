I'M GENERALLY AN active person. I strength train at the gym three days a week, along with a weekly Lagree class that never fails to kick my ass. But when cardio gets mentioned, catch me hiding behind the dumbbell rack.

I do like walking—but I haven't prioritized it as part of a dedicated routine. Embarrassingly, most of my walking is on my commute to work. For the next eight hours, I’m chained to the desk, other than the occasional 10-second walk to the coffee machine. During a typical week, I’ll accumulate between 1,000 to 3,500 steps Monday to Friday. I convinced myself that since I spend longer at the gym and occasionally give in to 10 minutes of hell on the StairMaster, that it's enough cardio. (It's probably not, if I'm being honest.)

[the Viral Walking Challenge](https://allpepay.com/)

Walking more has always been on the back of my mind. I did a 10K walking challenge last year, and it gave me the reality check that I generally sit on my butt for a ridiculous amount of time. After the 30-day challenge, I kept moving, but my step count hasn't stayed consistent. So when my editor asked me to take on another viral walking routine—the "6-6-6 Challenge" that made waves on my TikTok For You Page—I was down to try.

A lot of walking trends on social media are meant to help people like me move more. Unlike the others that promised weight loss or muscle gains, however, this one centered consistency.

What is the 6-6-6 Walking Challenge?

[to Build More Consistency](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/5977791954.html)

The 6-6-6 walking challenge involves three components within its daily regimen. First is six minutes of warmup stretches followed by 60 minutes of walking, and six minutes of stretching again. Ideally, these walks are slotted into your schedule at either 6 a.m. or 6 p.m., just to keep with the theme.

At first glance, these numbers seem like a catchy way to name the trend. But Nick LeClair, PT, DPT, clinical director at ATI Physical Therapy, explains to me, each has a purpose. “The warmup helps your body ease into movement, the steady walk builds endurance, and the cooldown helps you recover so you’re more likely to feel good afterward and stick with it over time.”

“What makes it appealing is that it gives people a simple, predictable routine,” Michael Wong, PhD, a kinesiology and nutrition sciences professor at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas tells MH. “A lot of individuals respond better to time-based prescriptions rather than general recommendations about step counts or intensity.” While the 10,000 step goal is largely overrated, reaching a certain daily step count is linked to a number of health benefits. Still, Wong says it's a moot point if you lack a consistent walking habit.

Importantly, the sessions should be more than a casual stroll. “The key to these walks is that they need to be brisk. That's how you get the benefit,” emphasizes W. Zach Smith, PT, DPT, founder of HIDEF Physical Therapy. “This will get the heart rate up into zone 2, which we now know based on the research is a great level of training for cardiovascular health.”

How I Approached the 6-6-6 Walking Trend

I did the 6-6-6 walking challenge for a month, from November 22 and December 22. To monitor my progress, I used my phone’s Fitness app to track my steps.

I wish I could say the walking itself was a delightful experience. Mostly, it was brick. Walking outside right as winter hit was, in hindsight, not fun. I would loop around the same block over and over instead of going to the nearby park. I wanted to be as close to my house as possible once the hour was up.

6-Minute Warmup

My typical pre-workout stretches included dynamic moves that typically included: low lunge to hamstring stretch, downward dog to plank to cobra, arm circles, and glute bridge. I wouldn’t normally stretch before taking a walk, but doing it helped me wake up and get into the mindset of movement.

60 Minutes of Brisk Walking

The brisk walking pace was not a problem. It was all I could do to keep warm in the freezing weather. I don’t have an exact number of how fast I paced my walking throughout the hour, I moved based on the idea of brisk walking. I walked to the pace where I was breathing deeply but could still holding a conversation with someone Though at 6 a.m., there was no one to talk to but myself.

My efforts paid off. In that one hour on the first day, iPhone’s Fitness app reported 6,240 steps at 2.88 miles. To put into context, the day before I had a grand total of... 609 steps. Not even half a mile. (In my defense, I went to the gym).

Of course the motivation to brave the elements wore off after Week 2 and beyond. Instead I walked up and down a hallway of my house, much to the exasperated looks of my family. Not my fault I have loud footsteps.

Since I wasn’t on a treadmill nor would I torture myself to wake up even earlier to haul ass to the gym, the brisk walking pace varied. Overall, my sessions ranged from about 4,200 to 6,300 steps during the 60 minute periods.

[Build More Consistency](https://memorycircuits.hbsnzj.com/)

One thing stayed consistent after every walking experience—I immediately had more energy. When I walked at 6 a.m., the grogginess faded away. If I walked at 6 p.m., it got me out of my evening slump.

Walking consistently also changed my state of mind. Starting the day already accomplishing a small goal kept me wanting to move more. I found myself finding any opportunity to walk and my daily step count would end up being between 8,000 to 12,000 steps. One day, I reached a total count of 17,190 steps. The only other time I've had a step count that high was when I traveled to Japan on vacation and walked everywhere.

My Honest Experience of the 6-6-6 Walking Trend

I enjoyed my time doing the 6-6-6 trend, but it's not a perfect walking strategy. In the beginning, it was good to have a strict time commitment. But my busy schedule made the 6 a.m./6 p.m. rule became inconvenient. Many times I was tempted to push walking to noon or 8 p.m.

Another issue I came across: since I commuted to the office three times a week, I needed to walk at 6 a.m. on those days. And it went against my night owl chronotype. Walking first thing in the morning messed with my sleep schedule. I use the sleep tracker app Rise Science, and it says I usually average seven hours of sleep. When I was doing the challenge however, I got less sleep on days I commuted. I was logging about four to six hours a night.

On the days I had shortened sleep, walking would give me energy to have a productive morning and afternoon. By evening I felt like a zombie. The rigidity of walking either at 6 a.m. or 6 p.m. messed with my gym schedule, feeling too exhausted to do my usual strength training.

Still, walking is an excellent activity for general health. Wong says consistently walking at a brisk rate creates meaningful cardiovascular and metabolic benefits. So did this translate to weight loss?

Nope. After triple-checking the scale, I actually gained a pound. It reminded me that weight loss is a complex equation. In my case, I hadn’t changed my diet or lowered my alcohol intake. I also started the challenge around Thanksgiving, an indulgent time of year to say the least.

All things considered, I would do the 6-6-6 walking challenge again. It gave me structure and helped me build a consistent habit. The only thing I would change is being more flexible on when I would actually walk, since movement, not rigid clockwatching, is the real priority. Walking one hour a day is way less daunting than hitting a specific number of steps, and from my experience, much more manageable.
