# This 'Immunity-Boosting' Peptide May Treat Long COVID. But the FDA Still Has 1 Major Concern.

ALMOST ALL OF the peptides sold on the gray market come with some questionable scientific data. As noted in MH’s peptide guide, a lot of health claims come from experiments that are designed poorly, work from a single lab with glaring conflicts of interest, or only feature testing in cells or animals. While preclinical studies have value, they are not a one-to-one replica of what could actually happen if the drug is injected into a living human being.

That’s what makes thymosin alpha-1 stand out. The peptide was first extracted from calf thymus in the 1970s and in the decades since has been subject to a number of studies—including human clinical trials. Scientists propose that thymosin alpha-1 mimics a natural peptide involved in modulating the immune system. Data shows that it works by enhancing antibody response and T-cell activity. Some studies suggest the peptide may also enhance the immune system’s ability to detect and destroy cancer cells before they spread, and it’s been explored as an add-on treatment alongside chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitors.

Thymosin Alpha-1 Up for Review in 2027?

ON FEBRUARY 2026, the FDA announced an advisory panel will review whether some of the restricted peptides should return to the Category 1 list. That’s not the same as FDA approval, but it would mean that pharmacies could compound them with a doctor’s prescription.

The advisory board is meeting for two sessions. The first session is taking place on July 23 and July 24, 2026, and will weigh evidence on seven peptides. While thymosin alpha-1 won’t be a topic for the first meeting, the peptide is expected to be brought up in a 2027 meeting.
