No matter which distance you're training for, a good plan includes three things: speedwork, longer and easier runs, and cross-training. A 30-minute sprint session one or two days a week is key for improving your overall fitness. Your speed and form will be better, and after giving these workouts your all, your race won't feel hard in comparison.

Longer, more relaxed runs will build your stamina and put you in the mindset you'll need to keep going on race day. They also help you burn fat. "At 60 minutes, your body starts to draw from fuels other than just your blood sugar; this process can help you lose fat faster," Tom Kloos, coach of the elite Bay Area Track Club, in 2015.

Training for a race isn't all about running though. Cross-training is important in building even more muscle (which will help you run faster), preventing overuse injuries, and promoting active recovery (which are lower-intensity exercises to get your blood flowing). Lifting, hiking, cycling, swimming, and even walking your dog are all great cross-training activities to incorporate into your routine.
