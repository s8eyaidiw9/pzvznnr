Constant Pain? Why Your Middle Ear Is Infected + Medically Approved Next Steps

If you're dealing with constant ear pain, pressure, or muffled hearing, a middle ear infection could be the cause. This condition—also known as acute otitis media—is common in both children and adults. While it can be uncomfortable and frustrating, most cases are treatable and improve with the right care.

Here's what you need to know about why middle ear infections happen, what symptoms to watch for, and what medically approved steps you should take next.

What Is a Middle Ear Infection?

A middle ear infection occurs when the space behind your eardrum (the middle ear) becomes inflamed and filled with fluid. This usually happens after a cold, sinus infection, or upper respiratory infection.

The middle ear connects to the back of your throat through a small canal called the Eustachian tube. When this tube becomes swollen or blocked, fluid gets trapped behind the eardrum. Bacteria or viruses can grow in that trapped fluid, leading to infection.

Why Does a Middle Ear Infection Cause Constant Pain?

The pain from a middle ear infection happens for a few key reasons:

Fluid buildup creates pressure behind the eardrum

Inflammation causes swelling and irritation

The eardrum stretches due to trapped fluid

In severe cases, pus may accumulate

Because the middle ear is a small, enclosed space, even a little swelling can feel intense. That's why the pain can seem constant or throbbing.

Common Symptoms of a Middle Ear Infection

Symptoms can range from mild to severe. The most common include:

Persistent ear pain (sharp, dull, or throbbing)

What Causes a Middle Ear Infection?

Most middle ear infections are caused by:

Viruses (such as those that cause colds or flu)

Bacteria that grow in trapped fluid

Allergies that cause nasal swelling

Sinus infections

Enlarged adenoids (more common in children)

Risk factors include:

Recent upper respiratory infection

Smoking exposure

Seasonal allergies

Being around others who are sick

Young age (children's Eustachian tubes are shorter and more easily blocked)

Is a Middle Ear Infection Serious?

In most cases, a middle ear infection is not dangerous and clears within a few days to a week.

However, untreated or severe infections can lead to complications such as:

Temporary hearing loss

Ruptured eardrum

Spread of infection to nearby tissue

Chronic ear infections

These complications are uncommon but possible. That's why ongoing or worsening symptoms should not be ignored.

Medically Approved Next Steps

1. Manage Pain First

Pain control is the first priority.

Doctors commonly recommend:

Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin)

These reduce both pain and inflammation. Always follow dosing instructions carefully, especially for children.

A warm compress placed over the ear may also help relieve discomfort.

2. Watchful Waiting (When Appropriate)

Not every middle ear infection requires antibiotics.

In many cases—especially mild ones—doctors recommend watchful waiting for 48–72 hours because:

Many infections are viral

The body often clears the infection on its own

Avoiding unnecessary antibiotics prevents resistance

However, this approach is usually only appropriate if:

Symptoms are mild

There is no high fever

The patient is otherwise healthy

Pain is manageable

3. Antibiotics (When Needed)

Antibiotics may be prescribed if:

Symptoms are severe

Pain is intense

Fever is high

Symptoms last more than 2–3 days

The patient is very young

There are signs of bacterial infection

If prescribed antibiotics:

Take the full course

Do not stop early, even if you feel better

Follow up if symptoms don't improve within 48–72 hours

4. Monitor for Warning Signs

While most middle ear infections resolve without serious issues, seek urgent medical care if you notice:

How Long Does a Middle Ear Infection Last?

Pain often improves within 1–3 days

Fluid may remain behind the eardrum for several weeks

Hearing may feel muffled until fluid fully clears

If hearing problems last longer than a few weeks, follow up with a healthcare provider.

Can You Prevent Middle Ear Infections?

While you can't prevent every case, you can reduce risk by:

Washing hands frequently

Avoiding close contact with sick individuals

Managing allergies

Staying up to date on vaccinations (including flu vaccine)

Avoiding cigarette smoke exposure

Treating sinus infections promptly

For people with repeated infections, a doctor may refer you to an ear, nose, and throat (ENT) specialist. In some cases, small tubes may be placed in the eardrum to prevent fluid buildup.

When Should You Speak to a Doctor?

You should speak to a doctor if:

Pain is moderate to severe

There is a high fever

Hearing loss is significant
