What Is Low Testosterone?

Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone. It plays a key role in muscle mass, bone density, fat distribution, red blood cell production, sexual function, and overall energy levels. It also influences mood, motivation, and cognitive function. (2)

Testosterone levels naturally decline with age, typically starting around a man’s early thirties. While this gradual decrease is considered normal aging, some men experience a more significant drop that leads to noticeable symptoms that could impact their daily life and performance. This condition is often referred to as low testosterone or testosterone deficiency. While ranges may vary slightly, testosterone levels below 300 ng/dL are considered low for most men, although other medical professionals don’t consider levels to be low unless they are under 250 ng/dL (3)

Distinguishing between age-related decline and a true testosterone deficiency is critical for understanding what your body is trying to tell you. Two men the same age may have completely different testosterone levels and symptom profiles. Lab testing and blood work can help confirm your exact levels, as symptoms alone cannot verify low T.

Physical Signs of Low Testosterone

Physical changes are often the first signs of low testosterone that men notice, even if they do not immediately connect them to hormone levels. Testosterone plays a major role in maintaining strength, lean muscle, and overall vitality.

Common physical symptoms of low T include: (4)

Chronic fatigue or low energy that does not improve with rest

Loss of muscle mass despite regular activity

Increased body fat, especially around the abdomen

Reduced strength and endurance

Decreased bone density, which can increase fracture risk

Joint pain and muscle aches

When testosterone is low, men often find that workouts feel harder and results come more slowly. Others notice weight gain without any changes in diet, or exhaustion even after a full night of sleep. Over time, these changes can have a major impact on men’s confidence, mobility, vitality, and overall health outlook.

How Low Testosterone Is Diagnosed

When clinicians diagnose low testosterone, they look beyond one single symptom. A holistic overview of a man's entire health outlook, combined with lab testing results, can help determine whether low testosterone is causing his symptoms.

The diagnostic process typically includes blood testing to measure total testosterone levels. If an initial testing result is low, repeat testing is often recommended to ensure the results are accurate.

In addition to lab work, a detailed symptom review helps determine whether low testosterone is the driving force of the issue. Combining objective data and clinical insight allows for a more accurate diagnosis and more effective individualized treatment plan.
