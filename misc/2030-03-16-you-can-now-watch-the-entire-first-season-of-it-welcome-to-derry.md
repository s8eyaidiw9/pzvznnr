WHEN PEOPLE SIT down to watch an HBO series, they expect one primary thing: quality. And for the most part, that's what they get—the prestige cable giant has built its reputation around its signature Sunday night programming and it doesn't tend to disappoint. What's rarer, though, is when HBO moves away from its typical dramas and dives into the genre space, horror in particular. While The Last of Us implements some horror elements (there are certainly zombies), the network's only other shows in recent history to tackle the spooky and scary were the Jordan Peele-produced Lovecraft Country and the Stephen King adaptation The Outsider—both of which aired in 2020, and both of which were cancelled after only one season.

It: A Novel

Welcome to Derry goes back in the timeline on Pennywise's story, heading out to King's fictional Maine town and letting viewers take in the scares from the comfort of their homes. Stephen King himself is a brand name, and the show is ostensibly connected to the It films (which combined to make over a billion dollars in 2017 and 2019)—so, it would seem like a safe bet that there will be a big audience eager for some Derry.

It helps, too, that Welcome to Derry is good. The show is based on select passages/"interludes" from King's It novel (a 1,000+ page doorstopper that was originally published in 1986), but generally charts its own path through Derry's spooky history. The first episode opens with some familiar territory (Derry kids being spooked by a missing friend and some very, very strange happenings) and some not-so-familiar territory (a military plot that promises to go to some very bizarre places). The show is unafraid to be violent, gnarly, and atmospherically spooky—and no one's life is off the table. Welcome to Derry has some truly shocking deaths, and they can come at any point. It's a clear message to viewers: Be constantly ready.
