MY SLEEVES ARE getting shorter, I can see the sun on both legs of my commute, and I’m tearing through tubes of sunscreen like a kid with Go-Gurt. That can only mean one thing: summer has officially arrived. But as you switch to an SPF moisturizer and start working out outside, don’t forget another seasonal upgrade—your scent. It’s time to trade your heavy cologne for something lighter, fresher, and made for the heat.

I love a deep, earthy cologne, but in the summer, it’s best to switch it up to match the brighter vibe of the season. Heavy, musky scents can feel overwhelming in the heat, so opting for something crisp and refreshing makes more sense. It’s also a great way to engage with the heat, as seasonal notes like citrus, neroli, lemon, or green tea can help you feel clean and energized, even on the hottest days.

That’s not to say all summer colognes need to be fresh or floral; there are certainly some amber and even woody options perfect for the warmer weather. But these won’t be the same ones you rock during the fall and winter. Changing your scent for the season simply keeps things interesting, so you can feel more in tune with the weather and time of year, even if you prefer the cooler months. The smallest tweak in scent can make a huge difference in how you carry yourself while basking in (or hiding from) the sun.

What to Consider

Fragrance Family

Fragrance families make it easier to narrow down the colognes you enjoy. Once you figure out which scent profiles you naturally gravitate toward, it helps cut through the overwhelming number of options. See, most colognes fall into four main categories: fresh, floral, woody, and amber.

While a single scent can blend elements from more than one family, nearly all of them stay rooted in one or more of these core groups. There is some debate over listing fougère as a fifth family (and you’ll see some brands do), but I treat it as a larger note that fits within the woody category. Here's how the four main break down:

Fresh: Fresh colognes burst with crisp, clean notes like citrus, green leaves, and gentle herbs. They’re energizing and airy, perfect for daytime wear or keeping cool in warm weather.

Floral: These colognes are sweeter, often highlighting notes like rose, jasmine, and neroli. They bring a softer, elegant edge to a fragrance in comparison to fresh scents.

Woody: Fragrance with warm, dry notes like cedar, sandalwood, and vetiver. These create a grounded, warmer feel that works especially well for evening wear or cooler weather (though there are some great ones for summer, but more on that shortly).

Amber: Amber colognes wrap you in rich notes like vanilla, resin, and warm spices, creating a cozy, lived-in vibe. Similar to woody scents, amber options are best suited for fall, winter, and evenings.

Type of Cologne

In addition to fragrance family, colognes are categorized by type, which is determined by fragrance concentration (how much perfume oil is in the formula). More than anything, this dictates how long the cologne will last. The three main types of cologne break down like this:

Eau de Cologne: The lightest option, Eau de Cologne, contains a low concentration of fragrance oils (usually between two and five percent), making it light, fresh, and short-lasting—typically just a few, like two to four, hours. Best for a quick freshening up.

Eau de Toilette: These contain around five to 15 percent of fragrance oil, offering a balanced, moderate scent that lasts about four to seven hours. It’s ideal for everyday use.

Eau de Parfum: These colognes contain 15–20 percent fragrance oil, giving them a rich, long-lasting scent that can linger for six to ten hours, and in some cases, even more. These are more concentrated and intense than Eau de Toilette and significantly more potent and longer-wearing than Eau de Cologne. You may also see Parfum, which is more intense, or Extrait de Parfum, which is the richest and longest-lasting scent.

Key Notes

Common summer top notes (aka the first thing you smell) include citrus scents like lemon, bergamot, and grapefruit, which provide a fresh and invigorating opening. Middle notes often contain herbs and florals such as lavender, rosemary, and jasmine, adding depth and complexity.

Base notes (the longer-lasting, deep scents in cologne that emerge after the top and middle notes fade), like musk, sandalwood, and cedarwood, then give lasting warmth and richness. For summer scents, light and refreshing notes such as citrus, aquatic scents, and green herbal elements are most popular because they evoke a clean, cool feeling suitable for warm weather. These bright and airy fragrances help keep you feeling fresh throughout hot days.

Size

Whenever I’m shopping for self-care products, I always consider size. With cologne, a few key factors help me decide whether to go big or small. First, I ask myself how often I’ll be wearing it—if it’s going to be my everyday scent, I usually opt for a larger bottle to make sure it lasts. Plus, cologne can get pricey, especially the stronger formulas, so buying a bigger bottle (over 3 oz) often ends up being more cost-effective in the long run. But if I’m just trying out a new fragrance or want something for a specific season (like summer), a smaller size (under 2 oz) makes more sense.

How We Selected

More than any other grooming or self-care product, cologne is subjective, leaving each individual’s fragrance palette up to personal preference. That being said, as a grooming editor, I’m constantly testing colognes of every fragrance family, from timeless scents to the latest releases, so I understand what makes a quality parfum. For this story, I considered a wide selection of scents across all fragrance families to land on the seven best seasonal options for men of all leanings. Below, the nine best colognes for summer 2026.

Tom Ford Neroli Portofino Eau de Parfum

Last winter, during one of those sub-zero freezing days, I found myself watching Call Me By Your Name, yearning for sunny, rural Italy. Smelling this cologne is the closest I’ve gotten to achieving that escape, and now that it’s beautiful again in New York (though nowhere near Italy’s level), it’s the perfect summer scent. It opens with vibrant citrus top notes of zesty bergamot and lemon. Then hints of neroli and orange blossom emerge, developing into a fresh floral brightness that evokes sun-drenched coastal air at the Italian Riviera. As it settles, warm amber and light musk then create a clean, luxurious trail that lingers.

It’s the embodiment of everything I love about warmer weather, offering a breezy, relaxed scent that simultaneously commands attention, the white linen suit of fragrances. Fresh colognes tend to be the quickest to fade, as their scent are lighter and highlight citrus, herbal, or aquatic notes, which evaporate faster than more intense woody or amber fragrances. But Neroli Portofino is an exception to this trend, offering day-long freshness. I’ve given myself a few sprays with this stuff in the morning, and it persists throughout the work day and into the night.

Bleu de Chanel Eau de Toilette

As one of the best-selling scents in the world, this fragrance earns its iconic status with a scent that smells great year-round, but works especially well for summer because of its bright, refreshing formula. I love how the fresh grapefruit, crisp cedar, and smooth sandalwood strike a perfect balance of being masculine without being overpowering. As a result, it’s my go-to for laid-back summer days when I just want to keep it subtle yet still sharp.

It’s crazy versatile, too. While it shines in daylight, it easily transitions into evening wear when the freshness fades, and warm sandalwood takes center stage. This cologne also evolves on your skin without losing its identity. It’s never loud, but always notable. It’s the kind of summer scent that stays fresh but leaves a quiet, confident impression wherever you go.

Aesop Virere Eau de Parfum

Aesop is hands down the best-smelling skincare brand for men, so it only makes sense that its cologne would be a favorite. There’s a reason why we gave it a 2025 Men’s Health Grooming Award shortly after it was released. When I wear this fragrance, it instantly transports me to taking a July stroll through Portland’s famed Japanese Garden, with every note capturing the fresh, natural energy of that moment in vivid detail.

The crisp bergamot and zesty petitgrain lead the way, unfolding into a calming blend of green tea, pink pepper, and cedar to create a nature-inspired aroma that feels both invigorating and grounded. While certainly on the shorter end for parfums, this cologne typically lasts me roughly six hours.

Imaginary Authors A City on Fire Eau de Parfum

I’m not against the beach or a picnic in the park, but for me, there is no better summer tradition than sitting around a campfire (preferably with whisky in hand). If you have a similar inclination during the warmer months, this is the scent for you. Never has a cologne captured the scent of warm smoke and charred wood more beautifully (or accurately) than City On Fire.

With top notes of red berries and spicy black pepper that evolve into a hazy burst of enflamed timber, it immediately transports you to sitting around a campfire with good drinks and better company, maybe somebody even has a guitar out, hopefully playing anything other than “Wonderwall”. Similar to the lingering scent of a fire, this fragrance persists for hours. While more subtle, I still smell it at least ten hours after applying, and often more.

While many athletes have recently started grooming brands, the trend for musicians seems to be launching new candles. Alicia Keys, Harry Styles, Troye Sivan, and Drake have all released their own candle brands in the last few years, with the latter rapper’s Better World Fragrance House being the best among the crop for men. The scents are sophisticated and cozy without overpowering their space, so when the brand dropped its first cologne last year, Summer Mink, I was certainly intrigued.

The fragrance opens with bright, herbal citrus before developing into notes of rose and jasmine. This blends with warm spices and then settles into a smooth, earthy base of patchouli. It’s a rare amber scent best suited for sunny days, like a bonfire on the beach. It’s ideal for warm weather celebrations and outdoor parties, with a scent that’s sure to stand out in a crowd without overpowering all those around you. It’s truly Drake’s best work in years, and probably would have saved him a lot of trouble if he had sent Kendrick a bottle.

Mind Games Blockade Extrait de Parfum

If you’ve ever taken a stroll through Central Park on a beautiful, sunny day, then you know the unexpected harmony between chess and summer. Mind Games, a fragrance line inspired by the classic game, was way ahead of the pair’s unique compatibility. Look no further than Blockade as proof. With top notes of mango, bergamot, pink pepper, and tomato leaf; middle notes of lavender and cyclamen; and base notes of amber, oud, and suede, it’s one of the most unique summer scents around.

Despite it traditionally being a staple of cooler seasons, the leathery finish works perfectly under the sun, like a great pair of loafers. Similarly, this fragrance is ideal for more formal warm weather fits, and my go-to for summer weddings. Not to mention, I get at least eight hours out of this stuff with a couple of sprays, so you don’t have to worry about a re-up mid-ceremony.

Arquiste Sydney Rock Pool

Last year, I took a trip to Cancun, and essentially spent three days alternating between diving into the water and sitting on the beach sipping tequila, drunkenly reading Scott Smith’s The Ruins (if you know, you know). At the end of each day, as I stumbled through the short walk back to my room, I had the same thought: I smell incredible.

See, not even the stench of overindulging in local spirits is enough to battle the uniquely delightful and refreshing scent of a day spent on the beach. Many colognes have tried to capture this in a fragrance, but Arquiste is the only one to truly nail it. With a deep seaweed and saltwater base that’s topped off with crisp coconut and smooth sandalwood, this storied scent perfects the aroma of fresh salt water and clean sand. I always find myself turning to this cologne whenever I’m either yearning for a tropical vacation, or just returned from one that never seems to be long enough.

John Varvatos Artisan Costiero Eau de Parfum

This scent from John Varvatos is barely three months old, but it’s already making a strong case to be one of my core summer scents in 2026. I can be a real basic bro with cologne, heavily favoring woody and musky scents, so I often struggle to find light or fresh fragrances to match the warmer weather. Artisan Costiero makes this challenge easy. Its earthy and aquatic scent opens with notes of citrus layered with aquacoral accord, akigalawood, and patchouli.

The inspiration of the Mediterranean sea can be smelt immediately, as this coastal scent thrusts you into the ocean waves with just enough woodiness to keep you afloat. It makes me feel like someone who would have actually joined Ernest Hemingway on his sailing expeditions, rather than just re-reading The Old Man and the Sea and dreaming about it. The ocean blue bottle is even topped with a wooden cap to evoke your finest handle of pirate’s rum.

Harry's Cowboy Classic Eau de Parfum

If your ideal summer involves hiking mountain valleys or roaming the desert, Harry’s Cowboy Classic is the cologne for you—especially if you don’t want to spend triple digits on fragrance. This scent feels like the olfactory version of Harrison Ford’s style: rugged, timeless Americana that pairs perfectly with denim. Base notes of musk, leather, and patchouli create a warm, grounded foundation reminiscent of a ranch at sunset, while bergamot, pink pepper, cedarwood, and amber add just enough brightness and spice to keep things interesting without overpowering the scent’s earthy core. It’s also surprisingly long-lasting for the price. You won’t get the 12-hour performance of a luxury parfum, but two or three sprays will comfortably carry you through seven or eight hours. For $35, that’s tough to beat.
