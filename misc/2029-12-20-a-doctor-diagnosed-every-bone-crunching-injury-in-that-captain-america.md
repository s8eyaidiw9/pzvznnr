Chris Evans performed a huge variety of stunts during his time playing Steve Rogers, aka Captain America, but one scene comes up again and again as the most memorable: the intense elevator fight from The Winter Soldier, which easily ranks as one of the best action sequences in the entire Marvel Cinematic Universe. Cornered by double agents in a glass lift at SHIELD headquarters, our hero quietly asks whether any of them would like to get off, before kicking each and every one of their asses.

In a recent video, Ed Hope, a YouTuber and doctor who works in emergency medicine, watches the scene and provides a blow-by-blow account of exactly how much damage Rogers inflicts on his opponents throughout the scene. "I would not like to be a first responder to this scene," he says. "It would be an absolute nightmare."

He assumes the absolute worst case scenario for each injury, as MCU lore states that Steve Rogers is as strong as a person can humanly be thanks to the super-soldier serum, not to mention all of his special training.

[Every Bone-Crunching Injury](https://innate-signal.jcesqc8.com/)

The very first hit of the fight, a kick to a HYDRA agent's knee, would probably result in a fracture and require surgery and rehab. "He's going to be out for six months," says Hope. Cap follows this by hitting another agent in the throat. "It's rare, but you can get a laryngeal fracture," he says. "Any trauma above the clavicle is potentially life-threatening, if blocking the airways. Even if he survived, we'd be looking at long-term dysphagia, difficulty swallowing, and dysphonia, issues with speech."

What follows is a series of blunt trauma to the HYDRA agents' faces and necks, which would leave them needing to be immediately immobilized in order to minimize the risk of further spinal damage, not to mention a surplus of severe head trauma; Hope says he would insist that everybody receive urgent CT scans to rule out epidural hematoma (a bleed inside the skull).

Then there are those who get a punch or kick to the torso, which could result in rib and clavicle fractures, trauma to the liver, and one of the enemy agents may even have gone into cardiac arrest, Hope explains, after being hit with an electrical current and losing consciousness.

[Elevator Scene](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/)

The agents who aren't immediately felled, or who bounce back for another round with Captain America, are rewarded with even more risk of serious brain injury, spine injury and facial fractures. "I think that's Captain America's M.O.," says Hope.

And even the hero of the piece doesn't make it out of the elevator unscathed; after Cap is hit repeatedly by Brock Rumlow (Frank Grillo) with an electro-shock baton, Hope concludes that he should have spent the rest of the movie nursing some bruising and minor burns, at the absolute least.
