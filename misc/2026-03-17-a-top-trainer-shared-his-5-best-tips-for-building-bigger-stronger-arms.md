# A Top Trainer Shared His 5 Best Tips for Building Bigger, Stronger Arms

While sculpting big guns might be a major focus for most guys in the gym, they don't always know the best ways to build muscle—especially when they've reached the limits of the standard dumbbell curl. In a new Athlean-X video, Jeff Cavaliere C.S.C.S. offers his best advice for growing arm muscle when you've reached a plateau.

While heavy compound lifts like bench presses, rows, and pullups have a part to play in progressively overloading your muscles and stimulating growth, Cavaliere explains there are other things you should be doing to complement these exercises and get the absolute maximum gains out of each and every arm workout.

Focus on Eccentrics

Cavaliere points out that often when training arms through weightlifting, people will prioritize the lifting movement but leave gains on the table by ignoring the benefit of the lower end of the movement. "If you look at a basic curl, we know we're lifting the weight up, but it's just as important to focus on slowing it down on the way down," he says. "Eccentrically, there's a lot of opportunity here for growth."

Keep Strict Form

Cavaliere explains that exercises that prioritize a perfect movement path, like the strict curl, performed with your back against a wall, can place greater pressure on the arms. "This is a great opportunity to force the work to be done just by the biceps; we're not allowing any momentum," he says. "But that doesn't mean we can't still have a relatively heavy load. Yes, it's less than what you can curl, but it's still a great load on the biceps, because they're handling all of it."

This principle can be adapted for other exercise variations, such as the incline overhead extension; having your back against the bench throughout the rep means you're not leaning forward and letting your upper body take some of that pressure off your triceps.

Don't Neglect the Brachialis

"You've got to train the brachialis heavy," says Cavaliere. "Perhaps here more than any other muscle in your arm, you have the opportunity to train with heavier exercises because it's not just the brachialis that works. It is a primary elbow flexor, but we also get help from the biceps and brachioradialis, so you want to take advantage of the fact that those muscles are working synergystically to give you the chance to lift more. Don't just relegate these exercises to the end of your workout."

He adds that exercises like hammer curls and pullups will hit the brachialis and train your arms from the inside out.

Add Extra Weight to Equipment-Free Exercises

Loading up your bodyweight movements with a weighted vest (or a full backpack if you're working out at home with minimal resources), can create opportunities for tension overload in the muscles, as opposed to simply adding repetitions to a weight you can already handle.

Working muscles brachioradialis is an important part of growing the size and shape of your arms overall, says Cavaliere, who recommends exercises which require a pronated grip position and maximum elbow flexion.

One move which includes both of these is the offset dumbbell curl, which focuses the load on the forearm. Cavaliere also recommends trying a curl using an EZ bar with a pronated grip that cheats toward supination with the curve of the bar.
