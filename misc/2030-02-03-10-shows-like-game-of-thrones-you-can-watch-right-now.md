# 10 Shows Like ‘Game of Thrones’ You Can Watch Right Now

It’s been over 250 days since the finale of HBO’s Game of Thrones, and whether you still can’t stop talking about it or can’t wait for people to stop talking about it, you know you miss it. And the data says lots of you still held onto your subscriptions, because, again: you miss it. Hoping to capitalize on your lovesickness, television producers have been churning out multi-million-dollar episodes in the form of The Witcher, See, and The Mandalorian. Apparently, what unites everything to Westeros is a trip to the Iron Bank.

But we all know it’s not just about the budget. What made GoT GoT included a mixture of traditional heroic tropes, contemporary antihero fun, and enough royal court intrigue and incestuous drama to make you scream REEK! While no one show combines all these ingredients in quite the same way, there’s more than enough series to make a throne claim.

Here are ten shows which will satisfy your craving for the high-stakes drama and quality storytelling that Game of Thrones did so well (well, at least in early seasons).

The Witcher

The massively expensive Netflix series based on the book series by Andrzej Sapkowski certainly looks like Game of Thrones: dark forests, dirty armor … shirtlessness. But the tone of the series varies slightly. Whereas Game of Thrones opted for an increasingly Jon-Snow-serious drama, The Witcher leans a bit more into the campiness of its fantasy genre, as well as the contrivance of its video game iteration; there are episodes in The Witcher that literally feel like side quests.

That silliness was by no means off-putting. In fact, we found it refreshing, even if it was also, at times, very very stupid.

Vikings

Miss all the blood and hard-to-pronounce Westerosian names? Meet Ragnar Lothbrok of Historychannel’s Vikings, a farmer turned warrior turned king-seeker. Filmed in Ireland just like its fantasy counterpart, Vikings is based on real-life Norse warriors and their warring across northern Europe. It’s a sprawling, generational epic that’s still kicking after six seasons. Netflix recently acquired rights for a sequel. So even when the series falls from History, it will never die.

His Dark Materials

Vivid characters, political intrigue, and sinister forces at play in a fantastical world — sound familiar? Based on Philip Pullman's beloved trilogy of novels, with an all-star cast including James McAvoy, Ruth Wilson and Lin-Manuel Miranda, His Dark Materials promises to be a television event of Westerosi proportions.

Taking place in an alternate, steampunk-tinged version of our reality, the story follows young Lyra as she rebels against her tutors and caretakers at Oxford, and stumbles across a conspiracy which is endangering other children and threatens to put the fate of the entire cosmos at risk. Also, did we mention there's a talking polar bear?

Netflix's first original series out of South Korea is a fun mash-up of historical drama and horror. Set in the medieval Joseon period, it opens as Crown Prince Yi-Chang begins to suspect his father, the Emperor, has fallen ill from a mysterious plague. What follows is an imaginative take on traditional vampire and zombie lore, as the virus travels across the empire while the prince, accompanied by a resourceful physician and mysterious outlaw, attempts to prevent it spreading and contends with a coup.

The Last Kingdom

Another historical fiction series, The Last Kingdom features all the English-inspired kingly drama and open field combat you crave. Set about two-hundred years before the inspiration for Game of Thrones (the War of the Roses), the series is based on The Saxon Stories, a collection of novels chronicling England’s Anglo-Saxon period of the ninth and tenth centuries. It’s an underrated show conveniently on Netflix. Add it to the old queue.

Succession

If your favorite parts of Game of Thrones were Tywin's scathing monologues, Varys and Littlefinger's sparring and various back-stabbing power plays in the capital, then Succession will scratch that itch. The Roy family give the Lannisters a run for their money in the ruthlessness stakes in this King Lear-inspired saga, created by Jesse Armstrong, who honed his craft writing for Black Mirror, Babylon and Veep.

Peaky Blinders

Loosely based on the 19th century English street gang “Peaky Blinders,” the series of the same name follows the fictional Shelby crime family of Birmingham. The series has all the family drama, Shakespearean betrayal, and witty dialogue that defined much of Game of Thrones’ early seasons. Unlike Game of Thrones, however, Peaky generally unites under one central character, played masterfully by Cillian Murphy. Maybe a bit more plot armor. Still loads of fun. Season 6 is filming now, so catch up while you can.

Watchmen

Adapted from the seminal comic series by Alan Moore, HBO is touting Watchmen as its Game of Thrones replacement, which makes a certain amount of sense. Just like George R.R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire, the source material here takes popular genre tropes (in this case, superheroes), tears them apart, and then weaves them back together into a dark and violent story with some big things to say about war, power, and morality.
