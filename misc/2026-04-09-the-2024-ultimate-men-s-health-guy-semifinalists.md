# The 2024 Ultimate Men’s Health Guy Semifinalists

WITH ALL THE awesome men who entered this year’s competition, judging was heated. These guys rounded out our top ten by living incredible, motivational lives.

10: Jordyn Pollack, 32

Entrepreneur and Photographer • Texas

Pollack transitioned in 2016 and has built a new body through lifting, running, skating, wakeboarding, and snowboarding. “Twice a week I do contrast therapy to help me relax and de-stress after a long day or week,” he says. And his friends help him wind down, too. “I am a very social person,” he says. “I love connecting with other transgender individuals through social media, as that has been amazing, meeting others who understand me.”

9: Duane Ehredt Jr., D.P.M., 40

Podiatrist • Ohio

[Men Health Guy Semifinalists](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

[The Ultimate Men Health](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/4457825816.html)

[Ultimate Men Health Guy](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/blog/3220839703.html)

In addition to his work as a clinician, Ehredt is an associate professor at Kent State University, a naval-reserve officer, and a dad. On top of all that, he’s always looking for a way to challenge his fitness—through a triathlon, trail race, or fitness test. How does he do it? By getting seven to eight hours of sleep, setting aside personal time, and planning. “One small thing I do every day is wake up early enough to have an undisturbed cup of black coffee. It helps me prepare. Making a plan/schedule and being disciplined also really helps me stay organized and on top of priorities.”

8: Carlvin Dorvilier, 33

[Guy Semifinalists](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/)

Google Business Lead • New Jersey

Dorvilier says his strength comes from not letting his past define him. “Overcoming the loss of my mother and financial hardship at a young age, I forged my own path.” Now he’s at Google as an agency business lead and cocaptains its internal organization for Black employees. To handle stress, he prioritizes exercise but also a mindful day-starting routine and journaling on Talkspace. “My journey has taught me that true manhood is not about machismo but about empathy, vulnerability, and defying societal expectations,” he says.

7: Nelson Candelario, 43

Attorney • California

[The Ultimate](https://antimicrobial-hub.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Candelario has been in the U. S. Marine Corps for 20 years and provides pro bono legal advice to veterans. He volunteers as an instructor of Brazilian jujitsu, a sport that he practices four to six times a week. How he stays grounded: “My family is central, and I prioritize quality time with them through activities like yoga with my wife and jujitsu with my two young boys.”

6: Christopher Borgstede, 60

[Guy Semifinalists](https://immunopeptide.xianfengchem.com/blog/2443678137.html)

Retired K-9 Police Officer • Idaho

Borgstede knows tragedy: injuries after hitting a deer at 60 mph on his motorcycle, surviving Whipple surgery to stave off pancreatic cancer, losing his ex-spouse to suicide. “Learning to lean on loved ones and faith in God was crucial,” says Gina Dado, Borgstede’s wife, who submitted his application. “Chris manifests what he wants to create in his life from a place of gratitude.”

5: Michael Twining, 56

Agricultural Sales and Marketing VP • Delaware

When Twining turned 50, he says he was in the worst shape of his life and thought mental health was taboo. “I had gotten used to letting life happen rather than making life happen,” he says. “Today, I feel like I’m just beginning to live.” Now the former Army officer exercises every day, cooks healthy, volunteers with a school in Uganda, and decompresses with his two rescue dogs.

4: Chris Ruden, 34

Professional Speaker and Author • Florida

[The Ultimate Men](https://memorycircuits.hbsnzj.com/blog/7596195528.html)

Born with two fingers on his left hand, Ruden hid his birth defect for 17 years. Through fitness, he found confidence—and the will to step forward into work as an advocate for disability inclusion. “I constantly do introspective check-ins with myself to manage the mental burnout that comes with life and a chronic illness.” At 19, he was diagnosed with type 1 diabetes, which he manages through a healthy diet and powerlifting.
