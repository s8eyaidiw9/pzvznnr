Microdermabrasion is a non-surgical and minimally invasive cosmetic treatment. The procedure has been shown to improve aesthetic appearance by reducing wrinkles. It’s also proven to be effective against minor scars, fine lines, and age spots.

PRP facial

A PRP facial is a natural treatment with a proven track record against wrinkles. It’s also been shown to treat sun damage, scars, and fine lines. It’s a non-invasive, non-surgical procedure.

How does PRP facial work?

The term PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma, which contains protein-releasing platelets. These active proteins boost skin healing and rejuvenation.

PRP therapy involves the extraction of platelet-rich plasma from your blood. It’s then infused back into the irregular part of your skin. It stimulates and increases the production of collagen and elastin. The end result is plumper and youthful skin.

Administration of PRP is usually done via an injection or microneedling.

The effects of PRP aren’t permanent. It’s a simple process, and most people see results after a few weeks.

Are PRP facials safe?

PRP facials are relatively safe. Temporary swelling, pain, and slight bruising are common minor side effects. These reactions go away a few days after treatment.

Avoid PRP if you have HIV/AIDS, blood cancer, or skin cancer. The procedure is also unsuitable for patients with hepatitis C and cardiovascular diseases.

Chemical peels

A chemical peel involves using chemical solutions to remove the outer skin layers. The result is the appearance of fresher, firmer, and smoother skin from underneath. It may also help with scars, acne, and fine lines.

How do chemical peels minimize wrinkles?

A chemical peel consists of a quick, minimally invasive, outpatient process.

Your physician starts by cleansing your face to remove excess oils. They then apply the solution. The chemical creates a controlled wound and penetrates into your skin. It peels away the old layers, revealing newer ones.

Chemical peels typically contain glycolic acid, salicylic acid, trichloroacetic acid, carbolic acid, or lactic acid. There are three types of chemical peels: mild peels, medium-depth peels, and deep peels.

Chemical peels are only effective against light wrinkles. The results may not be satisfactory for severe wrinkles.
