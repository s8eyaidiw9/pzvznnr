# Causes, Symptoms, Treatment, and Prevention of Ear Infections

28 Aug Causes, Symptoms, Treatment, and Prevention of Ear Infections

Ear infections, also known as middle ear infections or acute otitis media, occur when the space behind the eardrum gets infected. Ear infections are usually viral or bacterial and are most common in children but can happen to anyone at any age. Ear infections are very common, so knowing how to identify and treat them in yourself and your children is essential.

It’s also important to know how to differentiate between ear infections and other conditions and how to deal with chronic ear infections. This article will contain this information and much more, so keep reading to learn everything there is to know about ear infections.

Understanding Ear Infections

Understanding the symptoms, causes, and types of ear infections will help you better understand how to treat and prevent them.

Ear Infection Symptoms

It’s crucial to know the signs of an ear infection so that you can start treatment immediately and help negate the unwanted symptoms.

Difficulty hearing

Unusual ear drainage

Extreme ear pain

Loss of appetite

In children and babies, it’s difficult to know if they have an ear infection or if something else is hurting them. Therefore, besides the symptoms above, you should watch out for the following signs of a toddler or baby ear infection.

High fever

Ear tugging or pulling

More crying than usual

Extra fussy

Poor balance

Trouble sleeping

Causes of Ear Infections

Ear infections are always bacterial or viral and often result from another illness, such as the cold, flu, or an upper respiratory infection. Ear infections can also occur due to smoke or seasonal allergies, but these are not as common. Ear infections happen when infected germs travel through the eustachian tube to the inner ear and cause an infection and swelling in the eustachian tubes.

Types of Ear Infections

Ear infections can be acute or chronic and can occur in the inner, middle, or outer ear. Each type of ear infection has a similar course of treatment and gets diagnosed the same way.

Ear Infection Ear Drops

Ear drops are antibiotics in liquid form that get dropped into the ear and can be more effective than oral medications. Ear drops can also be antifungals and are great for reducing pain, irritation, and other unwanted ear infection symptoms.

To apply ear drops, lie on your side and pull gently on the bottom of the ear to open your ear canal. Next, have someone drop the ear drops into your ear and remain lying on your side for a minute to give the drops time to work their way into your ear.

Ear Infection vs. Swimmer’s Ear

It’s common to mistake an ear infection for Swimmer’s Ear. Both conditions cause a great deal of pain in the ear, but there are subtle differences. The pain caused by Swimmer’s Ear will be more in the outer ear, while an ear infection is in the middle and inner ear. Additionally, while home remedies and antibiotics are enough to treat ear infections, you may also require steroids to treat Swimmer’s Ear.

Dealing with Chronic Ear Infections

If you or your child has ear infections that are recurring and never seem to go away, this is known as a chronic ear infection. Doctors will use a steady dose of antibiotics to treat chronic infections and over-the-counter medications to deal with pain. Surgery to remove enlarged adenoids and tonsils or to install small tubes within the eustachian tubes is also an option for chronic ear infections.

How to Prevent Ear Infections

Because they often occur due to another condition or illness, practicing good hygiene is key to preventing ear infections. To do this, avoid the cold and flu by washing your hands, not spreading germs, and getting flu vaccinations. Additionally, because smoke can cause ear infections, steer clear of primary or secondhand smoke.
