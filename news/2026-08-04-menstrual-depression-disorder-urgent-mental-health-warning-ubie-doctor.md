Menstrual depression disorder, also known as premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), is a severe mood condition that causes intense sadness, anxiety, irritability, and anger in the days before your period. Unlike typical PMS, these emotional symptoms are strong enough to disrupt daily life, relationships, work, and overall well-being.

Because PMDD symptoms overlap with other mental health and hormonal conditions, identifying the true cause is essential to getting the right care. The sooner you understand what's driving your symptoms, the sooner you can take steps toward relief. Explanation

Menstrual depression disorder, often known as premenstrual dysphoric disorder, can have a big impact on mental health. This condition makes you feel extremely sad, anxious, or irritable in the days leading up to your period.

These strong emotions can make everyday tasks—like school, work, and spending time with friends—feel overwhelming. You might feel tearful or hopeless, and sometimes your mood can change quickly. It's different from the common premenstrual syndrome because the feelings are much stronger and can last longer.

The intense mood swings and depression can affect how you think and act, sometimes causing trouble concentrating or making decisions. Although it can be very challenging, there are treatments available such as counseling, lifestyle changes, and sometimes medication that may help reduce symptoms.
