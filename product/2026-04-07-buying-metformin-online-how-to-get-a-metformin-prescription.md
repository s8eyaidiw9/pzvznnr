METFORMIN AND AGING

How Metformin Works

When you eat, your body breaks down food into glucose, which is shuttled into the bloodstream. In response, your pancreas pumps out a hormone called insulin, which transports glucose into your cells where it’s used for energy.

Metformin works in a few ways to improve this process, which is what makes it a go-to drug for treating diabetes, where the body either doesn’t produce enough insulin or it has stopped responding properly to it, a condition called insulin resistance.

Metformin:

Lessens insulin resistance, so the body can absorb and use blood sugar more easily.

Reduces how much sugar is absorbed by your gut, so less sugar enters your bloodstream.

Lowers the amount of sugar produced by your liver and helps increase insulin sensitivity.

Beyond blood sugar regulation, studies have shown that metformin may also:

Increase the production of molecules in cells that signal the promotion of healthy aging (5)

Help with DNA repair and protect against DNA damage from oxidative stress (6)

It’s these combined powers that make metformin attractive if you’re looking to keep a healthy body weight, sidestep age-related illnesses, and live longer.

Benefits of Metformin for Non-Diabetics

It can help you avoid prediabetes

More than 1 in 3 Americans has prediabetes—blood sugar levels that are elevated, but not high enough to be considered diabetes. For many people, prediabetes turns into diabetes in time; metformin can help prevent this progression.

It may prevent cancer

Scientists are still trying to figure out exactly how metformin reduces the risk for certain types of cancer, including liver, pancreatic, and colon.

What they know so far: Like other cells, cancer cells require glucose for energy. If your glucose is managed, your risk for certain cancers may go down. Beyond lowering blood sugar, metformin appears to have other powerful anti-tumor effects (7).
