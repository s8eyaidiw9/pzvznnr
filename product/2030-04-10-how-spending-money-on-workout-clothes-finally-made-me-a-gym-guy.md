I’m your run-of-the-mill millennial guy: I’m addicted to coffee, I’m fine with avocados, and I’ve held memberships to no less than 12 gyms in my life without using them.

Keeping with the flaky, commitment-phobic stereotype of my generation, I’ve gone to these 12 gyms all across the northeast a total of maybe 20 times. But after receiving a wedding invitation to San Miguel De Allende—complete with a pre-ceremony pool party—my wife and I decided it was time to shave off some pounds and not look like two sacks of ground turkey meat.

She jumped right into the new fitness commitment, but I was, once again, overwhelmed. I blamed myself for my inability to latch on to a consistent exercise schedule, but the truth is I was never comfortable, physically or mentally, working out in the gyms I stubbornly kept paying to ignore. I always got too hot, I was always too thirsty, and my feet killed at the end of each and every treadmill session. Why would I go if I always felt like I had wasted my time and looked and felt like crap to boot?

Still, that destination wedding loomed over me. I was preparing to go through the familiar motions of joining a gym and dropping in once every three or four weeks when a story about water bottles caught my eye. For some strange reason I didn’t totally understand, I absolutely had to have it. I didn’t know it at the time, but the purchase of that one water bottle sent me on a journey of (relatively expensive) self-discovery that led me to morph from a delinquent gym membership holder to a guy that actually goes to the gym pretty often. What I didn’t realize then was how little I was prepared and how easy it is to buy appropriate gym gear.

Buying My Way Into Exercise

It all started with this Lifefactory 16-Ounce BPA-Free Glass Water Bottle, an accessory I have become totally dependent on.

It’s crazy to look back and think that I tried to push through entire workouts without proper hydration, but the minute I lugged this puppy into Crunch, everything changed.

Now that I stopped quitting halfway through my gym sessions to go home to guzzle agua, I started to ask myself what else I needed to feel more comfortable with myself for the gym. Turns out I needed a lot.

The next step was a bag. A huge amount inspiration came from Mack Weldon, a company I’ve followed for a while and purveyors of my personal favorite white t-shirt in the game. In the past I had used bulky backpacks to carry my gym gear, but it wasn’t until I got Mack Weldon’s Weekender bag that I actually felt inspired to bring my gym stuff to work.

This thing is the freakin’ Rolex of gym bags: high-capacity mobile device charger, water-resistant fabric with waterproof zippers, and eight pockets. It’s versatile, it’s sexy, and I felt really cool slinging it over my shoulder.

The bigger—and costlier—challenge was actually filling this bag up.

Dressing Up to Work It Out

If you Google “what do I wear to the gym,” dozens of articles pop up with dos and don’ts for guys exactly like me. They’re filled with a whole lot of don’ts I wasn't quite aware of: Don’t workout in a swimsuit, don’t wear loose clothing, and don’t run in street shoes.

Gulp. Not only was I guilty of all of these sins, but I actively rejected the athleisure culture that’s exploded out of gyms into everyday life. Like most unfounded pet peeves, this one was particularly dumb.

So, I went back to Mack Weldon and raided their online store: Loose 4-Way shorts, moisture wicking and anti-odor shirts, and even athletic socks with extra compression to help me on the treadmill.

I tossed my Vans to the curb, grabbed some shoes from York Athletics and found out what it felt like to run in running shoes. Shameful, I know, but what a difference! It sounds basic, but I truly felt like a whole new person in the gym.

Playing Mind Games

While I was loving how everything felt, part of me couldn’t help but think I was being ridiculous spending money on clothes I could be getting for cheap at Old Navy. I talked it out with a few experts, though, and I found out I was onto something.

“There is definitely a connection between buying workout gear, clothes, and shoes and going to the gym,” personal trainer, weight loss and wellness coach, and powerlifter Robert Herbst told me.

“A number of studies have shown that people are more likely to stick to an exercise routine or diet if there is a financial penalty for noncompliance. If someone spends a lot on workout gear or clothes, they may feel compelled to go to the gym and use it to justify the expense or else they will feel guilty. Getting people motivated to exercise can be complex, but any rationale that gets them there is worth it.”

Herbst’s point about “any rationale that gets them there” is what I would frequently come back to after obsessively checking my bank account balance. Because, the fact is, this was all working: I went from going to the gym no times a week to stopping in every other day. I’m no mathematician, but that seems like progress—and the changes already happening to my body are further proof.

“Workout gear only works when you use it,” said Melody Schoenfeld from Flawless Fitness. “Putting on new workout clothes or shoes can make you feel better about your appearance, or more motivated to sweat in your new duds, or might just make you feel like since you made the investment, you might as well get dressed and go pump some iron. So don't let your new clothes end up crumpled up in the corner of your closet!”

My gym clothes are being put to good use—and they have kicked off a new cycle of fitness-centric spending. I started investing in more cross training shoes, protein powder, and swanky-ass dopp kits to make the gym feel like a place I could strut around without feeling like a little milquetoast boy.

Now, a few months later and a few pounds lighter, the results speak for themselves. I actually only lost 3 lbs. because for once in my life I've gained muscle, and the gym gear I have fits even better.

More importantly, the residual sticker shock that first pushed me out of the house now serves as a reminder of the commitment I made to myself, my wife, and that pool in Mexico. I'm not so nervous about spending too much money now (within reason), because I'm getting plenty in return.

I’m gonna look look like a golden god at this Mexican pool party wedding.
